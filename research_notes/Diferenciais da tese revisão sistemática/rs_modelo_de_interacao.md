# Systematic review: agent–environment interaction model with hidden symbolic state, event-triggered abstract operators and RL low level

Search date: 2026-10-09. Reviewer: Claude (automated agent). Window: 2000–2026.

Thesis features compared against (the "differential"):
(F1) state (c, x), where c is a hidden complete STRIPS theory and is NOT a function of the observable x.
(F2) observation ψ = Φ(c, x) ⊆ c, partial and truthful (three-valued: true / false / unknown).
(F3) consistency with an abstract domain D_Γ: each change of c is either no change or exactly one operator application.
(F4) "events" (x_e, a_e, x'_e, ω_e), known a priori, trigger abstract operators.
(F5) low-level navigation learned by RL (DQN/HER).
(F6) BFS over a replay buffer H to build paths to an event while avoiding triggering other events.
(F7) families of environments generated by hyperparameters θ that share D_Γ.
(F8) goals arrive in sequence and knowledge persists between them.

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

**Protocol (fixed before searching)**
- Research question: has any work (2000–2026) proposed an agent–environment model in which the symbolic/abstract state is hidden and is not a function of the current observation, is revealed partially and truthfully, changes only by single abstract-operator applications triggered by known low-level events, and is combined with RL-learned low-level control? Secondary features were F6–F8.
- Inclusion criteria: (I1) published 2000–2026 (peer-reviewed, workshop or arXiv); (I2) combines a symbolic/abstract model (STRIPS/PDDL/SAS+/FOND, reward machine/LTL automaton, or a symbolic graph) with sequential decision-making by an agent; (I3) addresses at least one of: hidden or partially observed symbolic state, incremental symbolic knowledge, RL-learned low level under a symbolic high level, families of environments, or replay-buffer graph search.
- Exclusion criteria: (E1) pure LLM-agent planning with no environment-level symbolic state model; (E2) purely continuous POMDP control with no symbolic layer; (E3) application papers (driving, etc.) whose only overlap is the generic term "hierarchical"; (E4) no accessible content beyond the title.
- Overlap verdict scale: **identical** (F1–F5 all present), **partial** (at least two core features among F1–F5, or F1/F2 in a different formalism), **superficial** (one feature or only the general theme).
- Tools actually used: WebSearch (standard and extended modes); alphaXiv `discover_papers` (paper discovery); alphaXiv `answer_pdf_queries` (full-text reader that also fetched non-arXiv PDFs and web pages, e.g. AAAI OJS, PRL workshop and the author's homepage); Consensus `search`, which failed with "You've used all 30 searches this month".
- Tried and blocked in this environment: WebFetch and curl to ojs.aaai.org (proxy 403 / DNS failure), api.semanticscholar.org, api.openalex.org, api.crossref.org, export.arxiv.org, dblp.org (blocked by the Anubis anti-bot page when fetched through the alphaXiv reader), mlanthology.org, research.ibm.com, liner.com and arxiv.org through WebFetch. ## Q2. PRIORITY: Rozek, Lee, Kokel, Katz & Sohrabi (AAAI 2024 SA) and its longer version

### Takeaway
Both the AAAI-24 student abstract (2 pages) and a longer workshop version were read in full: Rozek et al., "Guiding Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning in Partially Observable Environments with AI Planning", PRL@ICAPS 2024, 6 pages. The work defines **PO-PaRL**. The abstract planning state is a **belief state computed as a surjective function L of the observation history**, not a hidden symbolic state. Knowledge of unknown variables is tracked by "discovery predicates", with possible discoveries encoded as FOND non-deterministic effects of existing operators. Options are learned by PPO. Environments are random seeds of one MiniGrid four-room layout. This is the closest work found, but overlap is **partial**, not identical.

### Cited Findings

**Bibliographic facts and what was read**
- AAAI-24 Student Abstract: Brandon Rozek (RPI), Junkyu Lee, Harsha Kokel, Michael Katz, Shirin Sohrabi (IBM Research AI), Proc. AAAI 38(21): 23635–23636, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i21.30504. Full text read through the alphaXiv reader at the OJS PDF — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504/32640); [article page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504)
- Author page lists the abstract (2024/03/24), a poster, and a longer workshop paper "Guiding Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning in Partially Observable Environments with AI Planning", PRL workshop 2024 — [Rozek publications](https://brandonrozek.com/publications/); [paper page](https://brandonrozek.com/paper/2403.01/)
- Longer version read in full (6 pages including appendix) — [PRL 2024 paper 12](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- Poster read — [AAAI poster](https://brandonrozek.com/files/research/AAAI_Remote_Poster_Rozek24.pdf)
- Rozek's 2024–2026 publication list contains no further follow-up on PO-PaRL. Later work is on qualitative possibilistic planning/goal recognition (ICAART 2026; HAXP 2024), logics and Spectra — [Rozek publications](https://brandonrozek.com/publications/)
- No arXiv/alphaXiv version was found. alphaXiv title resolution returned an unrelated paper (Lecerf et al., 2204.06509).

**(a) Is the symbolic state hidden or a function of observations?**
- It is a function of the observation history. PO-PaRL is defined as "the triple ⟨M, Π, L, D⟩ with POMDP M, FOND planning task Π, surjective function mapping history of observations to an abstract planning state L, and discovery set D" (Definition 1) — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- AAAI SA: "In the POMDP setting, the agent does not have access to a state. In order to derive our planning belief state s′, we need to pass a history of observations h into a mapping function L_h", with I_o := {s | L_h(h) ⊨ pre(o)} — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504/32640)
- The planning model represents the agent's knowledge state, not the world state: "the intuition … is that the agent's knowledge of unknown state variables are fully observable. In other words, the agent knows what it does not know" — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- The values of unknown variables (key colour/location) are hidden in the world until discovered. False and unknown are distinguished by discovery predicates: "if v ∈ s′0 … true … Otherwise … either false or unknown. To disambiguate between false and unknown, we make use of discovery predicates" — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504/32640)
- The parent PaRL framework uses L : S → S′, a surjective mapping from MDP states — [PaRL arXiv 2203.00669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00669)

**(b) How is knowledge updated?**
- Through discovery effects. Each discovery tuple d = ⟨o, V_o, F_o⟩ augments a deterministic operator o with oneof(e_{o,1}…e_{o,n}). Example: after (move-room r1 r2), key k1 may be found to be yellow in r2, purple in r2, or "nothing" — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- There are two constraints. A discovery tuple can fire only once (to "prevent cyclic policies"), and discovered variables must currently be unknown "to prevent inconsistencies in the planning state" — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- A FOND planner (PRP, Muise et al. 2014) produces an abstract policy. When L(h) is not covered by that policy (for example, exogenous discovery), the agent replans from the current abstract belief state. If no policy exists ("observability dead-ends"), it takes random primitive actions — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- Known limitation (sensing failure): the empty discovery effect is a subset of the others, so options can terminate prematurely. Example: the agent enters a room, does not see the key, and concludes there is no key. A later observation can then contradict the plan, which "will violate our policy, since the compilation assumed a deterministic observation model"; the authors rely on replanning — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504/32640); [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- Unlike contingent→FOND compilations (Albore et al. 2009; Bonet & Geffner 2011; Muise et al. 2014), "sensing outcomes may fail as the planning model may be incomplete with respect to the POMDP" — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)

**(c) Is the low level learned?**
- Yes. "We use the stable-baselines3 library … in which the underlying PPO implementation is used to train each of the options". Reward is −0.9 when the prevail is violated and 1 − 0.4·(t_o/H) on option success. The input is a stack of the last 10 observations (3×3 egocentric view, 270 dimensions). The comparison is against flat PPO and A2C; training used 8M samples over 10 runs — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- The poster summarises: "Train a RL-PPO Agent on each High-level Action … Penalize agent for deviating but not for discovering" — [poster](https://brandonrozek.com/files/research/AAAI_Remote_Poster_Rozek24.pdf)

**(d) Families of environments?**
- Only random variations of one task: four rooms in a 2×2 layout, start top-left, goal bottom-left, a yellow key needed for a locked door. "We randomly sample the agent direction, position, key locations, door locations, and goal locations"; 4000 seeds (3900 train / 100 test) — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)
- There is no sequence of goals and no persistence of knowledge across goals or episodes. The FOND policy is computed per task. The results are reported as preliminary ("may lead to improved performance") — [AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504/32640)

**Related work cited by Rozek (relevant to positioning)**
- Contrasted against reward machines under partial observability (Toro Icarte et al. AIJ 2023, which needs a labelling function from observations) and HIP-RL (Gordon, Fox & Farhadi 2019), whose "planner controller treats the problem as fully-observable and relies on replanning" — [PRL 2024](https://prl-theworkshop.github.io/prl2024-icaps/papers/12.pdf)

### Inferences
- Against the thesis features: **F1 no.** Their planning state is L(h), a deterministic function of the observation history, so it is an epistemic state rather than a hidden world theory c. **F2 partially.** Discovery predicates give a known/unknown distinction similar to three-valued ψ, and observations are assumed truthful, but they do not formalise ψ = Φ(c, x) ⊆ c. **F3 no.** There is no consistency condition; PaRL's "proper" condition is not restated for PO-PaRL. **F4 partially, and reversed.** Their operators are triggered by learned options reaching termination sets defined via L(h), not by known low-level events. Discovery is a non-deterministic effect of operators, whereas in the thesis observation is a separate channel. **F5 yes** (PPO options). **F6 no. F7 weak** (seeds of one layout and one PDDL instance). **F8 no.**
- The most important conceptual difference to state in the thesis is this. In PO-PaRL, uncertainty is compiled into the planning model (FOND with discovery effects), and the planner reasons over epistemic outcomes. In the thesis, c is part of the environment's true state, and partial observation ψ is separate from operator dynamics. Their acknowledged failure mode (premature termination when sensing fails, with a model that "assumed a deterministic observation model") is exactly the issue that a truthful-partial-observation model with persistent knowledge would address.

### Gaps
- The AAAI SA and PRL versions do not report numeric success rates in text; only plots were available, and their values were not extractable.
- No journal or full-conference version (2024–2026) was found on the author's page or through searches S14–S17.

## Q3. Snowballing (backward and forward)

### Takeaway
Backward snowballing from PaRL, Rozek and Li et al. added HIP-RL, Toro Icarte et al. 2023, Verginis et al. and the contingent→FOND compilations. Forward snowballing of PaRL could only be done indirectly (title searches and alphaXiv recency discovery). It found Rozek et al. (AAAI-24 SA and PRL-24) and the PaRL NeurIPS-2023 workshop version. It did not find any other paper that extends PaRL to hidden state.

### Inferences
- **No study is identical.** The defining combination F1 + F3 + F4 is absent everywhere: a hidden complete STRIPS theory c that is not a function of x, changing by one operator per known low-level event.
- The positioning closest to the thesis can be stated in three parts. (i) PaRL's "proper" condition shows that a consistency notion exists, but it is over L(s) and the MDP is fully observable. (ii) PO-PaRL moves the uncertainty into the planner (FOND with discovery effects over L(h)) and suffers from sensing failures. (iii) Noisy-RM work (Li et al. 2024) makes the symbolic state truly hidden, but uses automata rather than STRIPS operators and does not use truthful three-valued observation.
- F6 (BFS over a replay buffer that avoids triggering other events) only has partial analogues (SoRB, BEAG). None of them uses avoidance of symbolic side-effects as the criterion.
- F8 (goal sequence with persistent knowledge) is shown by InK (2026), but for unknown dynamics in a fully observable setting.

The main threats to validity are that bibliographic and citation APIs were blocked and that the Consensus quota was exhausted. Forward snowballing is therefore incomplete, and the counts are tool-level link counts rather than database hits.
