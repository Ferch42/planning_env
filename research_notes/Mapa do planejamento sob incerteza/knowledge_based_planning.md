## 1. PKS: exact bibliographic data, the databases Kf/Kw/Kv/Kx (+LCW), sensing, disjunction, soundness vs. incompleteness

### Takeaway
PKS represents the agent's *knowledge state* (not the world state) as a small set of STRIPS-like databases — Kf (known literals/function values), Kw (know-whether formulas, produced by binary sensing), Kv (know-value function terms, produced by value sensing), Kx (exclusive-or of ground literals) — each with a fixed translation into a first-order modal logic of knowledge; actions are add/delete updates on these databases, conditional plans may branch only on Kw (or, from 2004, on Kv of finite-range functions), and the query/inference algorithm IA is sound but deliberately incomplete because general (non-exclusive) disjunctions can neither be stored nor be created.

**Core idea**
- "Actions are then represented as updates to this collection of formulas. Hence, actions are being modelled in terms of how they modify the knowledge state of the planner rather than in terms of how they modify the physical world." — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- PKS "is based on a generalization of STRIPS. In STRIPS, the state of the world is represented by a database and actions are represented as updates to that database. In PKS, the agent's knowledge (rather than the state of the world) is represented by a set of databases" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)
- Rationale: an agent "without a model of uncertainty" "can only build plans based on what it knows and on how its knowledge will change"; planning at the knowledge level "abstracts away from a number of irrelevant distinctions" and supports functions and run-time variables, impossible in propositional possible-worlds planners — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Constants are rigid (KB contains (∃x)K(x = c) for each constant c), functions may be non-rigid; identical domains in all worlds assumed — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)

**The four databases and their modal-logic translation (AIPS-02, formulas (2)–(5))**
- **Kf**: "much like a standard STRIPS database, except that both positive and negative facts are allowed and we do not apply the closed world assumption"; any ground literal with constant arguments, plus function values f(c1..cn)=c / ≠c. Translation: for ℓ ∈ Kf, KB ⊨ K(ℓ). — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- **Kw**: conjunctions of atomic formulas (possibly with free variables) "every instance of which the agent either knows or knows the negation"; translation (∀x̄).K(φ(x̄)) ∨ K(¬φ(x̄)). Models plan-time effect of binary sensing; with variables it models universal sensing, e.g. UNIX `ls(d)` (local-closed-world info à la Etzioni, Golden & Weld 1997: "Kw can be thought of as a plan time analog" of LCW). Rigid predicates (=, <) are implicitly in Kw. — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- **Kv**: unnested function terms whose value will be known at execution time; translation (∀x̄)(∃v).K(f(x̄) = v) (standard knowing-value formula from Scherl & Levesque 1993). Models sensors returning constants/numbers (run-time variables, e.g. `readCombo; dial(combo())` in Moore's open-safe). — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf); [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)
- **Kx** (new in AIPS-02, not in KR-98): entries (ℓ1|ℓ2|…|ℓn) of ground literals meaning "exactly one of the ℓi is true"; translation K(∨i (ℓi ∧ ∧j≠i ¬ℓj)). — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Primitive queries: K(α), K(¬α), Kw(α), Kv(t), and their negations (α ground atom, t variable-free term); in 2004 written K(ℓ), Kval(t), Kwhe(ℓ). — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf); [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)
- Knowledge state semantics: the agent's knowledge state is "the set of all models that satisfy the formulas in KB in the style of Levesque's characterization of All I Know (Levesque 1990)" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- **LCW / fifth database**: AIPS-02/ICAPS-04 have four databases; LCW is handled via Kw with variables. A search summary reported later PKS descriptions speak of five databases (adding local closed-world knowledge, "LCW") — [search summary citing KVP IROS 2013](https://vigir.missouri.edu/~gdesouza/Research/Conference_CDs/IEEE_IROS_2013/media/files/1117.pdf) — **[unverified: I could not open a primary text naming the fifth database]**.

**Actions, update rules, consistency rules, branching**
- Planning problem = ⟨I, G, A, U⟩; preconditions and conditional-effect conditions are conjunctive sets of primitive queries; effects are add/del on any database; U = domain-specific update rules C ⇒ E acting as "state invariants at the knowledge level", triggered in any knowledge state where C holds (e.g. K(broken(x)) ∧ K(dropped(x)) ⇒ add(Kf, fragile(x))). — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Example of knowledge-level effect: drop(x) adds onfloor(x), dropped(x), deletes ¬broken(x) from Kf, and only if K(fragile(x)) adds broken(x). The authors note that after drop the agent actually knows K((broken∧fragile) ∨ (¬broken∧¬fragile)), "our representation cannot capture this information as it does not fit our syntactic restrictions. This is an example of where our representation is abstracting away from fine distinctions." — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Domain-independent consistency rules: (1) no α and ¬α both in Kf; (2) functions single-valued in Kf; (3) a literal added/deleted in Kf by a *non-sensing* action removes all Kx formulas mentioning it; (4) a literal added to Kf by *branch assumption* resolves Kx (ℓi true ⇒ other ¬ℓj added; ¬ℓi ⇒ shrink the xor). "Sensing actions add formulas to Kv and Kw, while ordinary actions add formulas to Kf." — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Algorithm PlanPKS: forward chaining; nondeterministically add an applicable action or branch on a ground atomic α from Kw (DB1: α→Kf, DB2: ¬α→Kf, α removed from Kw); goal must hold at every leaf. Implemented with BFS and DFS, "blind search, with some forms of cycle checking"; blind DFS "does surprisingly well". UNIX example (find paper.tex given Kx(indir(paper.tex,planning)|indir(paper.tex,kr))) solved by BFS in 0.16 s. — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Branching only on Kw "satisfies one of the important conditions for plan correctness in the context of incomplete knowledge put forward in (Levesque 1996)" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)

**Soundness / incompleteness**
- IA "is specified and shown to be sound [in Bacchus & Petrick 1998]. That is, every inference is correct and is entailed by the knowledge state KB. IA is, however, incomplete … We do not have a formal characterization of when IA is complete, but in all of the planning domains we have experimented with IA's incompleteness has not proved to be an impediment" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- Representational incompleteness: with worlds w1:{P(a),Q(b,c)}, w2:{P(a),¬Q(b,c)}, w3:{¬P(a),Q(b,c)}, "Since our representation does not allow us to model knowledge of general (non-exclusive) disjunctions such as K(P(a) ∨ Q(b,c)), we cannot represent the knowledge state characterized by the set {w1,w2,w3}" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf)
- "The computational efficiency of the approach … results from restricting the types of knowledge that can be expressed and the power of the inferential mechanism. In particular, only limited types of disjunctive knowledge can be represented and the inferential mechanism is incomplete." Higher-level representation "is richer, but the inferences it supports are weaker" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)
- Design philosophy: "motivated more by the ability to automatically generate 'natural' plans … than by the ability to generate all possible plans" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)

**ICAPS-04 extensions**
- (a) **Postdiction** (Sandewall 1994) over linearizations of the conditional plan with 4 rules (persistence forward/backward when a cannot change φ; forward application of known conditional effect; backward: effect ψ→φ observed activated ⇒ ψ held before; observed not activated ⇒ ¬ψ held before). Sound under the Markov assumption (complete knowledge of effects; agent is sole cause of change). Restricted to literals and to actions with at most one conditional effect per fluent "hence we cannot use these rules to infer arbitrary new disjunctions" (with two effects a→F, b→F, the correct inference would be a ∨ b). Closure in O(n·d²) rule tests (n leaves, depth d). Example: ⟨pour-on-lawn; sense-lawn⟩ yields Kw(poisonous). With two liquids, "The disjunctive conclusion that one of the liquids is poisonous cannot be represented by PKS". (b) **Temporally extended goals** Q^N (final), Q^0 (initial; restore goals), Q^* (all states; hands-off goals); goals may now combine queries with disjunction, conjunction, negation, limited ∃. (c) **Numeric expressions** (C-like) evaluable at plan time only. (d) **Kx over finite-range functions** (f(x) = {hi|med|lo}) enabling **multi-way branching on Kv** of finite-range functions. Search still blind; many examples solved in < 1–2 ms. — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)
- Comparison vs. possible-worlds planners (SGP/Anderson-Weld-Smith, MBP/Bertoli et al., CFF/Brafman-Hoffmann): those use "propositional representations over which complete reasoning is feasible" but cannot express run-time-variable plans like `dial(combo())`; conversely "There are also a number of examples of plans that can be generated by 'propositional possible worlds' planners that cannot be found by PKS" — [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)

### Inferences
- PKS's Kf is literally a 3-valued (true/false/unknown) partial state over ground literals plus partial function values; Kw/Kv are *plan-time placeholders* for knowledge to be obtained at run time; Kx is the only disjunctive structure, and it can only shrink (consistency rule 3 deletes it after ontic change) — so PKS never *creates* disjunctions, which is the root of its incompleteness.
- Incompleteness is two-layered: representational (some knowledge states inexpressible) and inferential (IA is incomplete even w.r.t. the KB it represents).

## 2. Formal foundations: Moore, Scherl & Levesque, Levesque 1996, Bacchus & Petrick 1998, Demolombe & Pozos Parra

### Takeaway
PKS's semantics is a restricted, syntactic fragment of the epistemic situation calculus line (Moore → Scherl & Levesque) with Levesque's "All I Know" and Levesque's 1996 conditions for plans with sensing; Bacchus & Petrick 1998 supplied the database representation and sound inference algorithm; Demolombe & Pozos Parra and Petrick & Levesque 2002 address the closely related idea of compiling world-level action theories into knowledge-level (literal-wise "knows p / knows ¬p") fluents.

## 4. Comparisons in the literature: PKS vs. 0-approximation / K0 / Palacios & Geffner / contingent planners

### Takeaway
Palacios & Geffner (JAIR 2009) explicitly position K0 as an automatic, heuristic-search-friendly realization of PKS-style knowledge-level planning: KL/K¬L fluents ≈ Kf positive/negative literals, K0 plans = conformant plans under the Baral–Son 0-approximation (3-valued states), all three are sound but incomplete because they discard disjunctive information; tags/merges (K_T,M, K_i) recover completeness for bounded conformant width, something PKS's Kx only partially addresses.

### Cited Findings
- Palacios, H. & Geffner, H. (2009). "Compiling Uncertainty Away in Conformant Planning Problems with Bounded Width." *JAIR* 35: 623–675. DOI: 10.1613/jair.2708 **[DOI from memory; volume/pages verified]** — [arXiv 1401.3468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- K0 definition: F′ = {KL, K¬L}; I′ = {KL | L unit clause in I}; G′ = {KL | L ∈ G}; preconditions L→KL; each conditional effect a: C → L becomes **support** a: KC → KL and **cancellation** a: ¬K¬C → ¬K¬L. "The translation K0(P) is sound as every classical plan that solves K0(P) is a conformant plan for P, but is incomplete." — [Palacios & Geffner 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- Proposition 3: "An action sequence π is a classical plan for K0(P) iff π is a conformant plan for P according to the 0-approximation semantics"; "both … throw away the disjunctive information"; with I = {p ∨ q}, neither sanctions a valid conformant plan. 0-approximation (Baral & Son 1997): "belief states b are represented by 3-valued states where fluents can be true, false, or unknown"; progression rule: L true after a iff some effect C→L has all of C true, or L true before and every effect C′→¬L has some literal of C′ known false. — [Palacios & Geffner 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- On PKS: "A sound but incomplete approach to planning with incomplete information is advanced by Petrick and Bacchus (2002) … while existing disjunctions can be carried from one belief to the next, no new disjunctions are added. … The two other limitations … are that domains must be crafted by hand, and that no control information is derived from the domains so that the search for plans is blind. Our approach can be understood as providing a solution to these two problems too: … the move to the 'knowledge-level' is done automatically, … solved by classical planners able to search with control information derived automatically." — [Palacios & Geffner 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)

### Inferences
- Mapping (my synthesis, consistent with the sources): Kf ∋ ℓ ⇔ Kℓ true in K0 ⇔ ℓ = true in the 0-approx 3-valued state; Kf ∌ ℓ, ¬ℓ ⇔ "unknown". PKS's `¬K(¬c) ⇒ del(Kf, ¬ℓ)` / `K(c) ⇒ add(Kf, ℓ)` effect pattern (poisonous-liquid / drop domains) is exactly K0's cancellation/support pair, hand-written by the modeller rather than generated.
- Where PKS goes beyond K0: Kw/Kv (sensing & branching; K0 is conformant-only, though its contingent descendants e.g. CLG/K-replanner add observation-based branching), Kv run-time variables, first-order/functional terms, Kx xor, postdiction rules. Where K0/K_i go beyond PKS: automatic translation, classical heuristics, and with tags/merges controlled reasoning-by-cases (completeness for bounded width) — PKS cannot represent K(P ∨ Q).

## 5. Relation to DEL epistemic planning, and to a (Π⁺, Π⁻) literal-theory agent with observation rules

### Takeaway
In Bolander's taxonomy PKS is the prototypical *syntactic* (knowledge-base) single-agent epistemic planner, dual to *semantic* DEL planning (epistemic models + action models, product update): syntactic approaches grow with knowledge, semantic ones with ignorance; DEL is far more expressive (multi-agent, higher-order) but plan existence is undecidable in general. A thesis agent that maintains (Π⁺, Π⁻) of known-true/known-false literals updated by observation rules is, formally, a PKS Kf database (equivalently a K0/0-approximation 3-valued state) with domain-specific update rules — it inherits soundness and incompleteness for the same reason (no disjunctions).

### Cited Findings
- Bolander, T. (2017). "A Gentle Introduction to Epistemic Planning: The DEL Approach." In S. Ghosh & R. Ramanujam (eds.), *Proc. M4M9*, EPTCS 243, pp. 1–22, doi:10.4204/EPTCS.243.1 — [arXiv 1703.02192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192)
- "It is also possible to take a syntactic approach, where states are represented as knowledge-bases, sets of formulas known to be true. … Syntactic approaches to epistemic planning can be found in e.g. the (single-agent) PKS planner, the (multi-agent) planning framework of [Muise et al. 2015] and the compilation approach of [Kominis & Geffner 2014]" — [Bolander 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192)
- "In a semantic approach, one is essentially modelling ignorance: the more ignorance, the bigger the state. In a syntactic approach, one is essentially modelling knowledge: the more knowledge, the bigger the state. Knowledge and ignorance are each others duals" — [Bolander 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192)
- DEL action models E = (E, (∼i), pre, post), product update ⊗; plan existence undecidable with two agents even without postconditions (Aucher & Bolander IJCAI 2013); decidable with propositional preconditions; PSPACE-complete with propositional preconditions and no postconditions (Charrier, Maubert & Schwarzentruber IJCAI 2016) — [Bolander 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192)
- Multi-agent syntactic descendants: Muise, Belle, Felli, McIlraith, Miller, Pearce & Sonenberg (2015) "Planning Over Multi-Agent Epistemic States: A Classical Planning Approach" (DMAP-15; AAAI-15 version); Kominis & Geffner (2014/2015) "Beliefs in multiagent planning: From one agent to many" — [Bolander 2017 refs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192). Recent DEL-planning work: DELPHIC (arXiv 2307.15451), depth-bounded epistemic planning (arXiv 2406.01139), EPDDL official guideline (arXiv 2601.20969, Jan 2026), Justified Perspectives (arXiv 2412.07981) — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969) (abstracts only).
- Knowledge-based programs as plans: Lang & Zanuttini (TARK 2013) "Knowledge-Based Programs as Plans: Succinctness and the Complexity of Plan Existence" — [Bolander 2017 refs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192); "Knowledge-based programs as building blocks for planning," *AIJ* (2022), doi:10.1016/j.artint.2021.103634 — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.artint.2021.103634) (authors not verified in-session).
- PKS's domain-specific update rules U ("state invariants at the knowledge level", e.g. K(broken)∧K(dropped) ⇒ add(Kf, fragile)) and postdiction rules are the PKS analogue of observation-driven inference rules — [Petrick & Bacchus 2002](https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/2002/AIPS02-022.pdf); [Petrick & Bacchus 2004](http://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/Papers/PetrickBacchusICAPS2004.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Π⁺, Π⁻) ↔ Kf⁺/Kf⁻ ↔ {KL}/{K¬L} in K0 ↔ 0-approx 3-valued state. Consistency requirement Π⁺ ∩ Π⁻ = ∅ ↔ PKS consistency rule 1. Observation rules that add literals to Π⁺/Π⁻ when the body is known ↔ PKS update rules U / support rules; rules that *remove* literals after ontic change when the condition is not known-false ↔ K0 cancellation rules / PKS `¬K(¬c) ⇒ del`. If the thesis's observation rules only fire on known-true bodies and only add literals, the update is sound (each added literal is entailed) but incomplete (cannot conclude ℓ from ℓ∨ℓ′ plus ¬ℓ′ unless that disjunction is stored — PKS adds Kx for exactly this xor case; K_i adds tags/merges).
- What the (Π⁺, Π⁻) theory lacks relative to PKS (if not added): a Kw-like "will know whether" set for plan-time reasoning about future observations (needed for contingent branching à la Levesque 1996), Kv for values, Kx for one-of constraints (e.g., "the hidden object is in exactly one location"), and postdiction (backward inference from observed effects to preconditions).
- Relative to DEL: the thesis representation is single-agent, first-order-knowledge only (no higher-order K_i K_j), and syntactic; a DEL model of the same hidden state would be the set of worlds consistent with (Π⁺, Π⁻) (all completions) — exactly the possible-worlds reading PKS uses (All-I-Know semantics), so (Π⁺, Π⁻) is a sound under-approximation of what the DEL/belief-state agent knows.
