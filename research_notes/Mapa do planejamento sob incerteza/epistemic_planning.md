# Epistemic Planning (DEL-based) and its bridges to knowledge-level / approximation-based single-agent planning

Scope note: this note covers DEL-based epistemic planning (states = pointed/multi-pointed Kripke/epistemic models, actions = event models, transition = product update), its decidability/complexity landscape, practical planners and languages (incl. EPDDL), and the bridges to (a) 0-approximation / K0 / Palacios & Geffner translations, (b) PKS, (c) contingent planning. Reader context: a single-agent formulation with known-literal sets (Π⁺, Π⁻) updated by observation rules, being compared with EPDDL.

## Foundations: the core DEL-planning papers

### Takeaway
DEL-based planning was introduced (essentially simultaneously) by Bolander & Andersen (JANCL 2011) and Löwe, Pacuit & Witzel (LORI 2011); the single-agent case is decidable while the multi-agent case is undecidable in general (Bolander & Andersen 2011 for 3 agents; Aucher & Bolander 2013 for 2 agents; Charrier, Maubert & Schwarzentruber 2016 already with modal-depth-2 preconditions). Andersen, Bolander & Jensen (JELIA 2012) give the single-agent, partially observable, conditional (contingent) version.

### Inferences
- Decidability/complexity landscape (synthesised; individual entries need checking against Bolander et al. AIJ 2020): single-agent DEL planning is decidable and its complexity matches classical (PSPACE) → conformant/contingent (EXPSPACE/2EXPTIME) planning; multi-agent becomes undecidable once event preconditions can have modal depth ≥2 or with unrestricted ontic+epistemic actions; decidable islands include public/propositional-precondition fragments and bounded-depth settings. The pivotal parameter for the reader's work is that **single-agent + S5 + public observations** sits squarely on the contingent-planning side of this map.
- For the reader: Andersen–Bolander–Jensen 2012 is the canonical citation for "single-agent DEL planning = contingent planning with knowledge-level conditions in plan branches".

## Practical planners and modelling approaches

### Takeaway
Practical epistemic planning splits into (1) **compilation to classical planning** over restricted, literal-based knowledge representations (Muise et al. RP-MEP/PDKB; Kominis & Geffner; Cooper et al. lightweight logic; Hu–Miller–Lipovetzky perspectives), and (2) **native DEL/Kripke search** (EFP/PG-EFP, EFP 2.0, MEPK, DELPHIC, DAEDALUS, newer heuristic-guided planners). Approach (1) is the direct relative of K0/PKS-style literal knowledge.

## EPDDL: the language, plank, and what it standardises

### Takeaway
EPDDL is by **Alessandro Burigana (Free Univ. Bozen-Bolzano) and Francesco Fabiano (Univ. Oxford)**, arXiv:2601.20969 (Jan/Feb 2026), the official language of the **first epistemic planning track at IPC 2026** (ICAPS 2026, Dublin). It is distinct from the earlier **E-PDDL** (2021). It standardises a full-DEL semantics via "abstract event models" plus a PDDL-style requirements system that names DEL fragments, with a baseline (`:pal`) that is only public announcements of conjunctions of atoms.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: Burigana, A. & Fabiano, F., "The Epistemic Planning Domain Definition Language: Official Guideline", arXiv:2601.20969, submitted 28 Jan 2026, revised 3 Feb 2026 (v2); CC BY 4.0 — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400236925_The_Epistemic_Planning_Domain_Definition_Language_Official_Guideline).
- Stated goals (verbatim): "1. Provide a clear and concise syntax for representing epistemic planning benchmarks; 2. Give a formal semantic foundation to the language based on Dynamic Epistemic Logic (DEL); and 3. Provide a standard way of defining DEL fragments, to enable meaningful comparison of different epistemic planners." Motivation: "existing epistemic planners often target different DEL fragments, and typically rely on ad hoc languages to represent benchmarks, and sometimes no language at all" — [guideline](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- Contributions: (1) *abstract event models* as semantics of epistemic actions; (2) formal syntax+semantics grounded in DEL — [guideline](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- **Observability types**: abstract frames are defined over a finite set ObsTypes (e.g., `Fully`, `Partially`, `Oblivious`); each action has an observability function assigning each agent an *observability condition* per type; conditions must be mutually inconsistent and cover the logical space, so each agent gets a unique, **state-dependent** type (e.g., `(?a (if (looking ?a) Fully else Oblivious))`) — [guideline §3](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- **Three-level structure**: problems, domains, and a new *action type libraries* component; an action type is a multi-pointed abstract frame (events, observability types, relations, designated events, event conditions); domains import libraries and supply pre-, post-, and observability conditions. The authors claim libraries can describe a wide range of DEL fragments and that non-DEL formalisms can be translated into DEL fragments representable by libraries (deferred to future work) — [guideline §4](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- **Baseline fragment `:pal`** (default requirement, analogue of `:strips`): (1) all formulas (pre-, post-, observability conditions, goal) are conjunctions of positive atoms; (2) all models are single-pointed S5ₙ models; (3) all actions fully observable; (4) all actions purely epistemic — hence only public announcements — [guideline §4.1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- **Requirements found in the text** include `:equality`, `:knowing-whether`, `:common-knowledge`, `:static-common-knowledge`, `:group-modalities`, `:negative-obs-conditions`, `:negative-goals`, `:negative-list-formulas`, `:lists`, `:list-comprehensions`, `:typing`, `:agent-groups`, `:facts`, `:finitary-S5-theories`, `:multi-pointed-models`, `:events-conditions`, `:non-trivial-postconditions`, `:non-trivial-events`, `:partial-observability`, `:ontic-actions` — [guideline §4](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969) (list extracted by grep; not guaranteed exhaustive).
- Initial states can be given explicitly as Kripke models (`:worlds`, `:relations`, `:labels`, `:designated`) or via finitary S5 theories — [guideline §4.3](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- **plank**: "the plank toolkit for DEL-based epistemic planning", public on GitHub, converts EPDDL into a simplified JSON format for ground tasks to feed planners — [guideline §1, §6](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969).
- Earlier, different language: **E-PDDL** — "E-PDDL: A standardized way of defining epistemic planning problems", arXiv:2107.08739 (July 2021; Udine, South Carolina, Union College, IBM), with parsers for leading multi-agent epistemic planners — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2107.08739). Author list reported as Fabiano, (Srivastava?), Lenchner, Horesh, Rossi, (Ganapini?) — **unverified**; workshop venue (KEPS/ICAPS 2021?) unverified.
- IPC 2026 epistemic track: located at ICAPS 2026, Dublin (June 26–July 2); organisers Burigana & Fabiano; feature stop 30 Apr 2026; planner abstracts 21 May 2026 — [call for planners](https://groups.google.com/g/icaps-conference/c/oImfNmuXKAk), [2nd call](https://groups.google.com/g/icaps-conference/c/J7QKb3Ghni4), [DMANET](https://dmatheorynet.blogspot.com/2026/02/dmanet-epistemic-planning-competition.html). A UVSQ news item reports that Halim Djerroud's planner **DEPTH** was awarded at the "first International Epistemic Planning Competition (IEPC 2026)" — [UVSQ](https://www.uvsq.fr/depth-recompense-lors-de-la-premiere-international-epistemic-planning-competition-iepc-2026?toPdf=true) (secondary source, page not fetchable; full rankings unverified).

### Inferences
- **Comparison point for the reader's (Π⁺, Π⁻) formalism**: in EPDDL terms, a single-agent formulation with literal knowledge + observation rules corresponds roughly to a single-agent task with `:ontic-actions`, `:partial-observability` (sensing = multi-event, multi-pointed actions where the agent distinguishes outcomes), `:knowing-whether`/negative goals — but the EPDDL *semantics* is exact product update on Kripke models, i.e., it tracks the full belief state (all disjunctive knowledge), whereas (Π⁺, Π⁻) is a literal-level (K0-like) projection. So an EPDDL encoding of the reader's domains would have the same plans whenever K0 is complete for them, and possibly more plans otherwise.
- EPDDL's observability *types with state-dependent conditions* are the multi-agent analogue of the reader's observation rules (condition → which agents learn what); in the single-agent case they collapse to "observes the outcome / does not".

## Bridges: DEL planning ↔ (a) K0 / 0-approximation / Palacios & Geffner, (b) PKS, (c) contingent planning

### Takeaway
The bridge is well established in spirit but rarely stated as a single theorem: a single-agent S5 epistemic model (up to bisimulation, with the actual world dropped) *is* a belief state; product update with an event model whose events are mutually distinguishable (public sensing) *is* belief progression plus observation filtering; so single-agent DEL planning with conditional plans = contingent planning (Andersen–Bolander–Jensen 2012; complexity ladder in Bolander–Jensen–Schwarzentruber 2015). The literal-level approximations (Son & Baral 0-approximation, PKS's K_f/K_w, Bonet–Geffner K-literals, the reader's (Π⁺, Π⁻)) are sound-but-incomplete projections of that S5 update; the translation-based methods (Palacios & Geffner; Kominis & Geffner) recover completeness by tagging literals with initial worlds, and Kominis & Geffner explicitly lift that trick from one agent to many.

### Inferences
(All inferences below are the note-writer's synthesis, not stated as theorems in a single cited source.)
- **Formal correspondence for the reader**: for a single-agent S5 model M (connected, i.e. one equivalence class), define Π⁺(M) = {p : M ⊨ Kp}, Π⁻(M) = {p : M ⊨ K¬p}. This is precisely PKS's K_f restricted to literals and the 0-approximation's state. It is sound (every literal in Π± is known in M) but forgets knowledge of disjunctions (K(p∨q) without Kp or Kq), so after a product update by an action with conditional effects or by a sensing event the literal sets can lose information that DEL keeps. K0-style planning is therefore *incomplete* relative to EPDDL/DEL semantics, exactly as 0-approximation is incomplete relative to possible-worlds semantics (Son & Baral).
- PKS's K_w ("knows whether") = DEL's Kw modality (EPDDL `:knowing-whether`); PKS sensing actions that add Kw(p) correspond to single-agent event models with two mutually distinguishable events (pre p / pre ¬p); PKS's run-time branching on K_w facts corresponds to Andersen–Bolander–Jensen's conditional plans.
- The Palacios–Geffner K_{T,M} tag translations are the classical-planning counterpart of keeping the full S5 model: tags = (sets of) initial worlds, K_t L = "L holds in all worlds that evolved from t". With a complete tag set (all initial worlds) the translation is exact for deterministic actions — the same move Kominis & Geffner use to track multi-agent accessibility.
- Observation rules "condition ⇒ observe literal" in the reader's model correspond to an event model whose events are the observation outcomes, with event preconditions = (condition ∧ outcome); when the agent always distinguishes outcomes this is single-agent "semi-public/partial observability" in EPDDL with only `Fully` as observability type.

### Gaps
- No source found (in this session) that proves a formal equivalence theorem "single-agent DEL planning with public observation ≡ contingent planning" or that formally relates S5 product update to K0; the search explicitly returned none. The correspondence is folklore/implicit in Bolander & Andersen 2011, Andersen et al. 2012, Bolander et al. 2015.
- No paper found explicitly relating PKS to DEL semantics (beyond PKS's own possible-worlds semantics in Petrick & Bacchus 2004); this is a potential contribution niche.
