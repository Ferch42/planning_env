# Knowledge-level, three-valued (approximate) and epistemic (DEL) approaches to contingent planning

Context: these notes support positioning "planejamento de teorias", where a state is a theory (Γ⁺, Γ⁻) of known-true and known-false propositions under an open-world assumption, operators update both parts, and observations add knowledge. Structurally, (Γ⁺, Γ⁻) is the "a-state" or 3-valued state of the 0-approximation (Son & Baral 2001), the K0 state of Palacios & Geffner, and the Kf database of PKS. Most of the formal results below therefore carry over to it directly.

Verification note: some publisher and author pages (aalto.fi, unimib.it, icaps-conference.org, rwth-aachen.de, arxiv.org) could not be fetched from this environment (DNS failures). Claims marked [abstract/secondary] come from search-engine abstracts or bibliographic records, not from the full text. Claims marked [full text] were read in the paper itself (through alphaXiv).

## Q1. Per-work: representation, update semantics, soundness/completeness vs possible worlds, complexity, when the approximation is complete

### Takeaway
The literal-based ("3-valued", "0-approximation", "K0", "knowledge-level") representation is a well-studied, sound but incomplete abstraction of belief states. Its main payoff is complexity: polynomial-time progression, and NP-complete rather than Σ₂P-complete plan existence for polynomial-length plans. It is complete only for restricted problem classes, for example conformant width 0 (Palacios & Geffner), or when the extra structure added (tags, sets of partial states, generalized formulas) covers the needed disjunctions.

### Cited Findings

**Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo (AIJ 2000): "Computational complexity of planning and approximate planning in the presence of incompleteness"**
- Exact citation: C. Baral, V. Kreinovich, R. Trejo, *Artificial Intelligence* 122(1–2): 241–267, 2000. A short version appeared at IJCAI 1999, pp. 948–953 [abstract/secondary] — [UTEP tech report record](https://scholarworks.utep.edu/cs_techrep/513); [Utah LNCS/bib index entry](https://ftp.math.utah.edu/pub/tex/bib/idx/lncs2000/1861/0/882_z.html)
- Setting: action language A (Gelfond & Lifschitz) and extensions with sensing. With complete information and polynomial-length plans, planning is NP-complete. With an incomplete initial state it becomes Σ₂P-complete. Under certain conditions, the 0-approximation of Baral & Son brings it back to NP-complete [abstract/secondary] — [UTEP tech report](https://scholarworks.utep.edu/cs_techrep/513)
- Tu, Son & Baral confirm this reading: the approximation-based approach has "low complexity in reasoning and planning tasks (NP-complete) comparing to those based on the possible world semantics Σ2P-complete (Baral et al. 2000a). The trade-off for this low complexity is incompleteness." Their NP-hardness proof reuses "Theorem 3 in (Baral et al. 2000a) which states that the conditional planning problem with respect to the 0-approximation ... is NP-complete" [full text] — [Tu, Son & Baral, TPLP 2007, arXiv cs/0605017](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605017)

**Son & Baral (AIJ 2001): "Formalizing sensing actions — A transition function based approach"**
- Exact citation: T. C. Son, C. Baral, *Artificial Intelligence* 125(1–2): 19–91, 2001. It defines the action language A_K with sensing ("determines") propositions and gives possible-worlds semantics, and also "several sound approximations that have much smaller state spaces" (the 0-approximation among them) [abstract/secondary] — [author PDF](https://www.cs.nmsu.edu/~tson/papers/SonB01.pdf)
- The 0-approximation in Son & Baral is the 3-valued idea originally attributed to Baral & Son 1997, which Palacios & Geffner cite as "(Baral & Son, 1997)" [full text] — [Palacios & Geffner 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- Precise update rules, as restated in TPLP 2007, extended there with static causal laws [full text] — [arXiv cs/0605017](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605017):
  - An a-state δ is a consistent set of literals, "the (possibly incomplete) current knowledge of the agent, i.e., it contains all fluent literals that are known to be true". Formulas are evaluated in a Kleene-style 3-valued way (T/F/unknown, with the strong-Kleene ∧, ∨, ¬ tables).
  - Non-sensing action a: e(a,δ) collects the effects whose conditions *hold* in δ (support). pc(a,δ) collects the literals whose conditions *possibly hold* (¬l ∉ δ), meaning they "may change". The result is Res(a,δ) = {Cl_D(e(a,δ) ∪ (δ \ ¬pc(a,δ)))}, which is deterministic (a single a-state).
  - Sensing action with determines(a,θ): Res(a,δ) = {Cl_D(δ ∪ {g}) | g ∈ θ, consistent}. This yields one branch per possible observed value, and inconsistent branches are pruned. This is the "observation adds knowledge" rule.
  - Proposition 1 (soundness, one step): for any state s ⊇ δ, the successor δ′ "is a subset of every state s′ ∈ Res^c(a,s)". In words, every literal known in the approximation is true in every possible successor world.
  - Proposition 4 / Corollary 2.1: Φ(a,δ) is computable in polynomial time, and plan verification is polynomial. Theorem 1: the conditional planning problem with polynomially bounded plan size is NP-complete.
  - Incompleteness example: D = {causes(a,f,{g}), causes(a,f,{¬g})}, I = ∅, G = {f}. Here [a] achieves f in every world, but it is not a solution under the 0-approximation, because the approximation "does not allow for reasoning by cases for non-sensing actions or in the presence of disjunctive initial situation."

**Eiter, Faber, Leone, Pfeifer & Polleres: language K / DLV^K**
- Part I: "A Logic Programming Approach to Knowledge-State Planning: Semantics and Complexity", arXiv cs/0112006 (Dec 2001). - Part II: "A logic programming approach to knowledge-state planning, II: The DLV^K system", *Artificial Intelligence* 144(1–2): 157–211, 2003 [abstract/secondary] — [record](https://datalearner.com:8443/academic/journal-papers/0004-3702/volumes-and-issues/268/paper-detail/1587)
- Representation: K describes "transitions between states of knowledge ... rather than transitions between completely described states of the world". **Petrick & Bacchus: PKS (AIPS 2002; ICAPS 2004)**
- "A Knowledge-Based Approach to Planning with Incomplete Information and Sensing", AIPS 2002. The knowledge state is a set of first-order modal formulas, and actions are "modeled in terms of how they modify the knowledge state of the planner rather than in terms of how they modify the physical world" [abstract/secondary] — [nmsu.edu copy](https://www.cs.nmsu.edu/~tson/classes/spring04-579/petricbacchus.pdf)
- "Extending the Knowledge-Based Approach to Planning with Incomplete Information and Sensing", ICAPS 2004. - Palacios & Geffner describe PKS as follows: "A sound but incomplete approach ... represent belief states as formulas. In order to make belief updates efficient ... while existing disjunctions can be carried from one belief to the next, no new disjunctions are added". **Palacios & Geffner (JAIR 2009): "Compiling Uncertainty Away in Conformant Planning Problems with Bounded Width"** [full text]
- Exact citation: H. Palacios, H. Geffner, *JAIR* 35: 623–675, 2009, doi:10.1613/jair.2708. The conference precursor is ICAPS 2007, "From Conformant into Classical Planning: Efficient Translations That May be Complete Too", and the paper also cites Palacios & Geffner 2006 for the L/X_i literals — [arXiv 1401.3468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468); [JAIR page](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10618)
- K0 (Definition 1): fluents KL and K¬L. The initial state contains KL for every unit clause L in I. Each effect a: C→L becomes a *support* rule a: KC→KL and a *cancellation* rule a: ¬K¬C→¬K¬L. Proposition 2: K0 is sound (every classical plan is conformant) but incomplete.
- **Proposition 3 (formal link to 3-valued semantics):** "An action sequence π is a classical plan for K0(P) iff π is a conformant plan for P according to the 0-approximation semantics". Furthermore, "L is true at time i according to the 0-approximation iff the literal KL is true in the state s_i". Both "throw away the disjunctive information".
- Disjunction failure example: with I = {p ∨ q} and a: p→q, the plan [a] is conformant for G = {q}, but neither K0 nor the 0-approximation accepts it.
- K_{T,M}: tags t (conjunctions of initially-unknown literals) give literals KL/t, meaning "if t is true initially then L", i.e. K(t₀ ⊃ L). Merges m (sets of tags with I ⊨ ∨_{t∈m} t) give merge actions ∧_{t∈m} KL/t → KL, which perform reasoning by cases. Theorem 7: K_{T,M} is sound if all merges are valid and all tags are consistent.
- K_S0 (one tag per possible initial state) is sound and complete but exponential. K_models is complete and can be exponentially smaller (Theorem 17).
- Conformant width w(P) satisfies 0 ≤ w(P) ≤ n, where n is the number of initially unknown fluents (Proposition 22), and it can be computed in time exponential in w(P) (Proposition 21). **Theorem 24:** "For a fixed i, the translation K_i(P) is sound, polynomial, and if w(P) ≤ i, covering and complete." K_0 = K0, so K0, and hence the 0-approximation, is complete for width-0 problems. "Most conformant benchmarks have width 1". The resulting planner T0 won the IPC-2006 conformant track.
- Comparison with sets of 3-valued states (Son, Tu, Gelfond & Morales 2005; Son & Tu 2006; the CpA planners): those use global contexts, with a number of partial initial states that can be exponential even at width 1, whereas tags encode local contexts.

### Inferences
- A (Γ⁺, Γ⁻) theory state with open-world semantics is isomorphic to a consistent literal set (a-state / K0 state): Γ⁺ = {p | p ∈ δ} and Γ⁻ = {p | ¬p ∈ δ}. The expected guarantees therefore follow: progression is sound and polynomial, plan existence is NP-complete for bounded plans (vs Σ₂P), and completeness holds only for width-0 problems or problems without disjunctive/case-based reasoning.
- If the thesis operators update Γ⁺ and Γ⁻ the way K0 does (add on known conditions, remove from Γ⁻ when a condition is possibly true), the soundness proof can mirror Palacios & Geffner Proposition 2 and Tu/Son/Baral Proposition 1. If an operator instead removes Γ⁻ entries only when the condition is *known* true, soundness breaks: this is the role of the cancellation rule ¬K¬C → ¬K¬L.
- The thesis observation rule (adding knowledge) corresponds to Son & Baral's sensing Res: branch on each sensed value and prune inconsistent branches.

## Q2. What exactly is lost by literal-based knowledge, and known fixes

### Takeaway
Literal-based knowledge loses all non-unit (disjunctive and conditional) knowledge: the initial clauses (p ∨ q), correlations created by conditional effects, and the reasoning by cases that combines them. Known fixes add (a) conditional literals KL/t with merges, (b) sets of 3-valued states, (c) limited disjunctions such as PKS's Kx, (d) more general tracked formulas (Fadnis & Rintanen), or (e) exact compact representations such as DNF/CNF/prime implicates or BDDs (To, Son & Pontelli).

### Cited Findings
- Both K0 and the 0-approximation "throw away the disjunctive information and restrict the plans to those that make no use of the uncertain knowledge". With I = {p ∨ q} and a: p→q, the valid conformant plan [a] for G = {q} is not found: "some ability to reason with disjunctions is needed" [full text] — [Palacios & Geffner 2009, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- The 0-approximation "does not allow for reasoning by cases for non-sensing actions or in the presence of disjunctive initial situation". The paper's example causes(a,f,{g}) plus causes(a,f,{¬g}) gives a tautological effect that the approximation cannot conclude [full text] — [Tu, Son & Baral 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605017)
- Fix 1, tags and merges (conditional knowledge K(t₀ ⊃ L)): KL/t literals plus merge actions ∧KL/t → KL. These are sound for valid merges, complete for K_S0, K_models, and K_i when w(P) ≤ i [full text] — [Palacios & Geffner 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468). Note the distinction ¬KL/t vs K¬L/t, i.e. "the conditional K(t₀⊃L) is not true" vs "K(t₀⊃¬L) is true" — same source, §4
- Fix 2, sets of 3-valued states (Son, Tu, Gelfond & Morales 2005; Son & Tu 2006; the CpA planners). Several partial states are progressed independently, and completeness needs case-splitting on the relevant fluents. The count can be exponential even at width 1 [full text, as described by] — [Palacios & Geffner 2009, §11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- Fix 3, PKS carries existing disjunctions but adds none [full text, as described by] — [Palacios & Geffner 2009, §11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3468)
- Fix 4, generalized 3-valued formulas: track the truth of derived formulas, not only literals [abstract/secondary] — [Fadnis & Rintanen 2022](https://users.aalto.fi/~rintanj1/papers/FadnisRintanen22.pdf)
- Fix 5, exact belief representations (minimal DNF, minimal CNF, prime implicates) inside a generic planner [abstract/secondary] — [To, Son & Pontelli 2015](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/725)
- Contingent extension: the contingent translation of Albore et al. tracks KL/t-style knowledge under sensing and is complete for bounded contingent width [abstract/secondary] — [IJCAI 2009](https://www.ijcai.org/Abstract/09/271). Belief tracking is exponential in width, and beam tracking is a sound approximation in causal width [abstract/secondary] — [Bonet & Geffner, JAIR 2014](https://mlanthology.org/jair/2014/bonet2014jair-belief)
- Logical diagnosis: by Ciucci & Dubois, a 3-valued (Kleene-type) logic of incomplete information is a fragment of epistemic logic with modalities only on literals. So it cannot express □(p ∨ q) ∧ ¬□p ∧ ¬□q, i.e. knowing a disjunction without knowing either disjunct [abstract/secondary] — [Ciucci & Dubois 2012, HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03413949)
- Kleene truth-functionality also gives p ∨ ¬p = unknown when p is unknown, even though it holds in all worlds. The 0-approximation's strong-Kleene ∨ table makes this explicit [full text] — [Tu, Son & Baral 2007, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605017)

### Inferences
- The thesis can frame (Γ⁺, Γ⁻) as the "MEL fragment with modalities on literals" (Ciucci & Dubois), i.e. K0 / 0-approximation. A natural extension that answers the examiners would be to allow Γ to contain clauses (as in PKS/Kx or Fadnis & Rintanen) or conditional entries L/t (as in Palacios & Geffner).
- The trade-off can be stated as a spectrum: literals (K0) ⊂ tagged literals K_i ⊂ K_S0 ≅ full belief states, with cost exponential in width.

### Gaps
- No source found that gives completeness conditions for the 0-approximation with *sensing* (contingent rather than conformant), beyond the width results of Albore et al. and Bonet & Geffner.

## Q3. DEL-based epistemic planning: relation to contingent planning, and is single-agent S5 DEL planning equivalent to contingent planning?

### Takeaway
Single-agent DEL planning with S5 (equivalence-relation) models and action models with ontic postconditions is, semantically, contingent planning over belief states. An epistemic model with designated worlds is a belief state with an observability partition, product update generalizes belief progression, and strong plans are contingent plans that work in all contingencies. Bolander (2017) states the induced-model correspondence explicitly, and Andersen, Bolander & Jensen (2012) frame conditional epistemic planning as planning under partial observability with strong and weak plans. Single-agent DEL plan existence is decidable, while multi-agent plan existence is undecidable in general. Literal-based states are the "syntactic/knowledge-base" pole that Bolander contrasts with the semantic DEL pole.

### Cited Findings
- Andersen, Bolander & Jensen, "Conditional Epistemic Planning", JELIA 2012, LNCS 7519, pp. 94–106, doi:10.1007/978-3-642-33353-8_8. "DEL offers a solid foundation for automated planning under partial observability and non-determinism". Strong plans must branch to guarantee the goal under all contingencies, while weak plans only make the goal possible. It gives a conditional plan language translated to DEL for verification by model checking, plus a tableau-inspired synthesis algorithm that is "terminating, sound and complete" [abstract/secondary] — [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/conditional-epistemic-planning/); [Springer DOI](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-33353-8_8)
- Bolander & Andersen, "Epistemic Planning for Single- and Multi-Agent Systems", *Journal of Applied Non-Classical Logics* 21(1): 9–34, 2011, doi:10.3166/jancl.21.9-34. Planning is decidable in the single-agent case and only semi-decidable (undecidable) in the multi-agent case [abstract/secondary + reference list in full text] — [Bolander 2017 references](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192); [DTU PDF](https://www2.imm.dtu.dk/~tobo/bolaea_epis.pdf)
- Bolander (2017), "A Gentle Introduction to Epistemic Planning: The DEL Approach", EPTCS 243: 1–22 [full text] — [arXiv 1703.02192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02192):
  - A belief state is a set of propositional states, and progression is γ(s,a) = {γ(w,e) | w ∈ s, e ∈ a, w ⊨ pre(e)}.
  - Belief states alone cannot separate plan-time from run-time uncertainty. In the single-agent case, states as sets of sets of worlds are "essentially equivalent to representing states as models of single-agent epistemic logic".
  - "If s′ is the epistemic state induced by a belief state s, and a′ is the action model induced by a propositional action a, then s′ ⊗ a′ is the epistemic state induced by γ(s,a)".
  - Solutions are strong policies, with weak solutions also discussed (footnote 13).
  - Complexity: plan existence is undecidable with 2 agents even without postconditions (Aucher & Bolander, IJCAI 2013). It is decidable with propositional preconditions ((n+1)-EXPTIME upper bound), PSPACE-complete with propositional preconditions and no postconditions, and NP-complete for some announcement-only classes (Bolander, Jensen & Schwarzentruber, IJCAI 2015).
  - Syntactic vs semantic: "PKS ... [and] the compilation approach of [21]" (Kominis & Geffner) are syntactic approaches. "In a semantic approach, one is essentially modelling ignorance ... In a syntactic approach ... knowledge"; the two are duals.
- Aucher & Bolander, "Undecidability in Epistemic Planning", IJCAI 2013, pp. 27–33. The result holds already in S5 with at least 2 agents, and with 1 agent in S4 [abstract/secondary] — [IJCAI](https://ijcai.org/Abstract/13/015)
- Kominis & Geffner, "Beliefs In Multiagent Planning: From One Agent to Many", ICAPS 2015, pp. 147–155. Single-agent belief-space planning is extended to many agents in a DEL fragment without event models or product update, with "the same complexity of belief tracking as the single-agent setting". ### Inferences
- Equivalence, single-agent S5. Suppose the action models are propositional (preconditions and postconditions are propositional), the agent's own actions are observed, and partial observability of outcomes or sensing is modeled by event indistinguishability. Then epistemic states correspond to belief states plus an observation partition, and strong DEL plans correspond to strong contingent plans (branching on observations). Bolander's induced-model statement and the "sets of sets of worlds" footnote support this. **I found no single theorem titled "single-agent S5 DEL planning = contingent planning"**, so present it as a well-known correspondence and cite Bolander 2017 §5–8 and Andersen et al. 2012. Differences: DEL allows epistemic preconditions and goals (e.g. K p ∨ K ¬p, "knowing whether"), and since S5 single-agent K-formulas reduce to belief-state queries, this adds convenience but no new single-agent expressiveness (inference). Contingent planning in the AI planning sense is usually EXPTIME- or 2-EXPTIME-complete for plan existence, depending on observability; this is from memory and not verified here, so check Rintanen 2004 (ICAPS) before citing.
- Where the thesis sits. (Γ⁺, Γ⁻) is a syntactic, literal-level knowledge base, i.e. the PKS / K0 / Muise-RML end of Bolander's syntactic–semantic axis. It can be presented as a *projection* of a single-agent S5 epistemic model, where p ∈ Γ⁺ iff M ⊨ Kp. Progression under K0-style rules is sound but incomplete (Palacios & Geffner Prop. 2–3; Tu, Son & Baral Prop. 1), which answers "Any formal result showing a literal-based knowledge state is the projection of a possible-worlds state and that progression is sound but incomplete". The closest formal statements are:
  - Tu/Son/Baral Proposition 1 (a-state successor ⊆ every successor world state).
  - Palacios & Geffner Proposition 2 (soundness) plus Proposition 3 (exact correspondence of KL with 0-approximation knowledge).
  - Ciucci & Dubois (3-valued logic as the literal-modal fragment of epistemic logic).
- Muise et al.'s RML approach is the multi-agent analogue of literal-based knowledge: it is also sound but incomplete with respect to full DEL, since it restricts to modal literals and closure rules (inference from the RML design, not verified in full text).
