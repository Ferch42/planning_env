## 1. How is contingent planning defined, and how does it differ from classical, conformant and FOND planning?

### Takeaway
Contingent (conditional) planning is propositional planning with (i) an *uncertain initial situation* (a set of possible initial states, often given as a formula or clauses), (ii) actions that may be deterministic or nondeterministic, (iii) *partial observability* through sensing actions or a sensor model, and (iv) a goal that must be reached with certainty. A solution must branch on observations. The variants differ along two axes: action determinism and degree of observability. Classical planning has one initial state and deterministic actions. Conformant planning has no observability, so the plan is a sequence. FOND planning has full observability and nondeterministic actions. Contingent planning (POND, partially observable nondeterministic) covers the cases in between and generalises the others.

### Cited Findings
- **Rintanen's general model [FT].** A problem instance is ⟨A, I, O, G, V⟩. A is a set of Boolean state variables. I and G are formulas describing the initial and goal states. O is a set of operators ⟨c, e⟩, where c is a precondition and e is an effect built from literals, conjunction, conditional effects c ▷ e, and nondeterministic choice e1 | … | en. V ⊆ A is the set of observable variables. The instance is *fully observable* if V = A, *unobservable* if V = ∅, and *partially observable* otherwise. Rintanen notes that observations that depend on the last action can be reduced in polynomial time to this fixed-V model. — [Rintanen 2004, "Complexity of Planning with Partial Observability", ICAPS-04, pp. 345–354](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Rintanen's plan definition [FT].** A plan is a finite directed graph ⟨N, b, l⟩. Operator nodes carry an operator and a successor node. Branch nodes carry pairs ⟨φ, n⟩, where the formulas φ may mention only observable variables V. A plan solves the instance if, in its execution graph over S × N, every (state, node) pair reachable from an initial state without passing through the goal can still reach a goal state at a terminal node. Read probabilistically, this means goals are reached with probability 1. Loops (unbounded executions) are therefore allowed, which is the strong-cyclic semantics. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Haslum & Jonsson's terminology [FT].** A *conformant* planner builds plans that work in every foreseeable case: every initially unknown value and every nondeterministic outcome. When sensing operators exist, *contingent* planners build branching plans. They borrow the MDP terminology: a domain is *fully observable* if there are precondition-free sensing operators for every proposition, *unobservable* if there are none, and *partially observable* otherwise. In unobservable domains contingent plan existence coincides with conformant plan existence. Their model is nondeterministic, not probabilistic: "not even a probability distribution over the possible outcomes is known." — [Haslum & Jonsson, "Some Results on the Complexity of Planning with Incomplete Information", ECP-99, LNAI 1809, Springer 2000, pp. 308–318](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/aiics/publications/ECP-1999-Some-Results-Complexity.pdf)
- **Albore, Palacios & Geffner [FT].** "Contingent planning is concerned with the problem of achieving goals in the presence of incomplete information and sensing actions" (citing Peot & Smith 1992; Pryor & Collins 1996). A contingent problem is P = ⟨F, O, I, G⟩: fluents F, actions O, I a set of clauses over F for the initial situation, and goal G ⊆ F. Actions are STRIPS with conditional effects C → L. Sensing actions obs(L) reveal the truth value of a literal L. The paper assumes uncertainty only in the initial situation and deterministic actions. — [Albore, Palacios & Geffner, "A Translation-Based Approach to Contingent Planning", IJCAI-09, pp. 1623–1628](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf) ([IJCAI page](https://www.ijcai.org/Abstract/09/271))
- **Bonet & Geffner's sensor model [FT].** A partially observable problem is P = ⟨F, O, I, G, M⟩. M is a sensor model made of pairs (C, L): the truth value of L is observable whenever C holds. A sensor can be read as an information-gathering action with precondition C that fires automatically. — [Bonet & Geffner, "Planning under Partial Observability by Classical Replanning: Theory and Experiments", IJCAI-11](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf) ([IJCAI page](https://ijcai.org/Abstract/11/324))
- **FOND as defined in PRP/PR2 [FT].** A FOND task is ⟨V, s0, s*, A⟩ with a *complete* initial state s0. Each action has a set of possible outcomes, and the realised outcome is observed after execution. Rintanen describes the same setting as nondeterministic actions with full observability, where plans must be "mappings from states to actions" or "simple programs with execution conditional on the nondeterministic outcomes". — [Muise, McIlraith & Beck, "PRP Rebooted: Advancing the State of the Art in FOND Planning", AAAI-24 (arXiv 2312.11675)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.11675); [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Knowledge-based sensing semantics [FT].** In the action-language tradition (Son & Baral 2001), a *k-state* is a pair ⟨s, Σ⟩: s is the real world state and Σ is the set of states the agent considers possible. A non-sensing action progresses both. A sensing action on f1…fk keeps s and filters Σ to the states that agree with s on f1…fk. Conditional plans branch on knowledge acquired at execution time. — [Baral, Kreinovich, Sarkar, Tran, Trejo & Zhang, "Complexity and approximation studies of finding polynomially bounded length plans for temporal goals", UTEP TR (2003 version), restating Son & Baral 2001, AIJ 125:19–93](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/2001/tr01-01f.pdf)
- **DEL connection (from EPDDL) [FT].** In DEL-based epistemic planning, nondeterminism and partial observability live inside epistemic states (pointed Kripke models with multiple designated worlds) and inside event models. The EPDDL guideline shows informally that any conformant task with possible initial states S can be encoded as an epistemic planning task whose initial state is the disjoint union of the states in S, with the same set of solutions. EPDDL is the official language of the first Epistemic Planning Track at IPC 2026. — [Burigana & Fabiano, "The Epistemic Planning Domain Definition Language: Official Guideline", arXiv 2601.20969 (2026)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.20969)

### Inferences
- A useful taxonomy for the report has two dimensions, *initial uncertainty / action determinism* × *observability (none / partial / full)*. Classical, conformant, FOND and contingent (POND) are cells of this grid, as in Rintanen's Table 1 (see §4).
- The student's "knowledge base with known-true / known-false / unknown literals" corresponds to the *knowledge-level* view (Petrick & Bacchus 2002; the KL/K¬L literals of Palacios & Geffner and Albore et al.; the T/F/U states of Haslum & Jonsson; the a-states and 0-approximation of Baral et al.). All are sound but generally incomplete approximations of the belief state (see §2).
- Single-agent DEL planning with S5 models and multiple designated worlds behaves like belief-state contingent planning: the set of worlds is the belief state, and sensing events partition it. This gives a formal bridge between the student's two frameworks.

- The PDDL extensions for contingent planning (`:observe`, `oneof`, `unknown`) are mentioned only indirectly (Eppe & Bhatt's PDDL-like syntax; Bertoli et al. 2002, NuPDDL). I found no primary specification.

## 2. Belief states and their representations

### Takeaway
In the non-probabilistic setting a belief state is a *non-empty set of states*, which is finite. There are 2^(2^n) belief states over n variables, which is where the jump in complexity comes from. A formula holds in a belief if it holds in every member state. Practical representations include explicit sets, BDDs (MBP), CNF/DNF or clause sets (Contingent-FF uses an implicit representation via the action history plus SAT), and compiled *knowledge-level literals* (KL / K¬L, possibly tagged KL/t). The literal-based ones are exact only under width or "simple problem" restrictions.

### Cited Findings
- **Beliefs as sets of states [FT].** "a belief state b is a non-empty collection of states, and a formula A holds or is true in b if A is known to be true in b, meaning that A holds in every state s ∈ b." The progression of b by action a is the set of results of applying a to each s ∈ b. The successors after sensing are "maximal set[s] of states in b that agree on the literals that are sensed." — [Bonet & Geffner, IJCAI-11](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- **Why non-probabilistic beliefs make the problem decidable [FT].** With goal probability 1, "the uncertainty about the current state can be represented in terms of a set of possible current states". In the probabilistic case beliefs are distributions, of which there are infinitely many, while "the number of non-probabilistic belief states, interpreted as sets of states, is finite." Over n variables there are 2^n states and 2^(2^n) belief states. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Search in belief space [SEC/FT].** Contingent planning is usually cast "as a non-deterministic (AND/OR) search problem in belief space [Bonet and Geffner, 2000; Hoffmann and Brafman, 2005; Bertoli et al., 2006; Bryce et al., 2006]". This is "the mainstream approach in both contingent … and POMDP planning [Kaelbling et al.]". Belief tracking is harder than state tracking, and searching for policies is harder than searching for sequences. — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf); [Bonet & Geffner 2011](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- **Symbolic (BDD) representation [SEC].** MBP represents sets of states and belief states symbolically with BDDs. The keywords of Cimatti et al. 2003 include "symbolic model checking, binary decision diagrams". Strong planning under partial observability with BDDs is treated in Bertoli, Cimatti, Roveri & Traverso, "Strong planning under partial observability", AIJ 170(4–5):337–384, 2006 (cited in Albore et al. 2009). — [Cimatti et al. 2003 record (FBK)](https://cris.fbk.eu/handle/11582/353)
- **Knowledge-level literal compilation [FT].** In the K_{T,M}(P) translation of Palacios & Geffner (2007, 2009), the fluents are KL/t: "it is known that if t is true in the initial situation, L is true". Merges (DNFs of tags) derive the unconditional KL. Each effect a: C → L yields a support rule KC/t → KL/t and a cancellation rule ¬K¬C/t → ¬K¬L/t. K_i is exponential in i and complete for conformant width ≤ i. The T0 planner (K1 + FF) won the IPC-2006 conformant track. — [Albore et al. 2009, §3](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf); [Palacios & Geffner, JAIR 35:623–675, 2009 (arXiv 1401.3468)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3468)
- **Contingent extension X(P) [FT].** Sensing obs(L) becomes the nondeterministic action ¬KL ∧ ¬K¬L → KL | K¬L, "capturing the effect of observing the truth value of L at the knowledge-level [Petrick and Bacchus, 2002]". Two deductive rules are added: Contingent Merge (∧(KL/t ∨ K¬t) → KL) and Tag Refutation (KL/t ∧ K¬L → K¬t). X(P) is sound. X_{S0}(P), with tags equal to all initial states, is complete but exponential. X_i(P) is polynomial for fixed i and complete when the *contingent width* w(P) ≤ i. Almost all benchmarks have width 1. — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf)
- **"Simple" problems: beliefs equal literals plus invariants [FT].** If the non-unary initial clauses are invariant and no hidden fluent appears in the body of a conditional effect (deterministic actions), then every reachable belief equals the set of states satisfying R ∪ I_u, where R is the set of known literals (Theorem 2). Known literals stay known (monotonicity, Proposition 3). The K′(P) translation (KL, K¬L; sensors become nondeterministic KC, ¬KL, ¬K¬L → KL | K¬L) is sound and complete for such problems and linear in size. The size of X(P) in general is O(|P|^(w(P)+1)). — [Bonet & Geffner, IJCAI-11](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- **T/F/U states (three-valued knowledge) [FT].** Haslum & Jonsson define a T/F/U description: each proposition is mapped to T if true in all states of the set, F if false in all, and U otherwise. A state set is a "T/F/U state" if it equals the realisation of its description, meaning every proposition is either known or independent of all the others. With a T/F/U initial state and T/F/U-preserving operators, non-branching plan existence drops to PSPACE-complete. — [Haslum & Jonsson 2000](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/aiics/publications/ECP-1999-Some-Results-Complexity.pdf)
- **0-approximation, three-valued and sound [FT].** Baral et al. use *a-states* (consistent sets of literals) and the 0-transition of Son & Baral. Formulas can be true, false or unknown. 0-approximate plans are sound but incomplete, and planning with them is NP-complete for polynomial-length plans, compared with Σ2P for exact conformant planning (see §4). — [Baral et al. TR (restating Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo, AIJ 122:241–267, 2000)](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/2001/tr01-01f.pdf)
- **Knowledge-state vs world-state encodings [FT].** Eiter et al. argue that *knowledge-state* encodings, where fluents can be explicitly unknown, can turn conformant ("secure") planning into ordinary ("optimistic") planning for problems like "bomb in the toilet". World-state encodings require the harder secure planning. — [Eiter, Faber, Leone, Pfeifer & Polleres, "A Logic Programming Approach to Knowledge-State Planning: Semantics and Complexity", INFSYS RR-1843-01-11 / arXiv cs/0112006 (2001); journal version ACM TOCL 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0112006)

### Inferences
- The student's high-level KB with three-valued literals is an instance of a *literal-based, knowledge-level belief approximation*: PKS-style (Petrick & Bacchus), 0-approximation (Son & Baral), K0 / K′ (Palacios & Geffner, Bonet & Geffner). The report can state precisely when such a representation is *exact*: Bonet & Geffner's "simple problems", or width-1 problems for X1(P). Otherwise it is sound but incomplete: it can miss plans or lose disjunctive information such as "x1 or x2".
- The monotonicity property ("known literals remain known") of simple problems is what makes replanning with a classical planner complete (on connected state spaces). This is relevant if the RL layer can make known facts unknown again, for example through exogenous change.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full text of Hoffmann & Brafman 2005 (Contingent-FF, ICAPS-05 pp. 71–80), which represents beliefs implicitly via action sequences and CNF reasoning, or of Bryce, Kambhampati & Smith 2006 (POND, JAIR 26:35–99). The descriptions of their representations here come from secondary citations.
- I did not verify Bonet & Geffner, "Belief Tracking for Planning with Sensing: Width, Complexity and Approximations" (JAIR 2014). The alphaXiv lookup failed. I believe it shows that belief tracking is exponential in a causal "width" parameter, but this is unverified.

## 3. What solutions look like: plan trees, policies, weak / strong / strong cyclic, offline vs online

### Takeaway
A contingent solution is a branching structure. It can be a *policy tree* (action nodes, with branching on observations), a *plan graph / finite controller* with loops, or a (partial) *policy mapping belief states to actions or action sequences*. For nondeterministic domains, Cimatti, Pistore, Roveri & Traverso (2003) fix three solution notions. A *weak* solution reaches the goal on some execution. A *strong* solution reaches it on all executions in a bounded number of steps (acyclic). A *strong cyclic* solution guarantees that every execution can always still reach the goal and that terminating executions end in the goal, which equals goal reachability under fairness, or with probability 1. Solutions can be computed *offline*, as full contingent plans whose size can explode, or *online*, by closed-loop replanning: compute a partial plan, execute it, observe, and replan.

### Cited Findings
- **Policy tree [FT].** "The solution to a contingent problem P is a contingent plan that can be regarded as a policy tree Π: a tree T with a function A mapping the internal nodes n of T into actions a(n)… a node n in the tree has two children when the action a = a(n) is a sensing action." Π solves P when the executions of all branches are feasible and end in belief states where the goals hold. The authors note: "Most contingent planners build graphs rather than trees, yet this distinction is irrelevant from a theoretical point of view." — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf)
- **Policies over beliefs [FT].** "The general solution to a partially observable planning problem can be expressed in many forms, the most common of which are trees of possible executions, and functions mapping belief states into actions." Bonet & Geffner use partial functions Π(b) → action sequences applicable in b. Π solves P iff every possible execution from the initial belief reaches a goal belief. — [Bonet & Geffner 2011](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- **Plans as graphs with loops [FT].** In Rintanen's general definition, plans are finite graphs of operator and branch nodes. A looping plan "has no finite upper bound on the length of its executions, but … with probability 1 eventually reaches a goal state". A non-looping plan has a finite bound on execution length. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Weak, strong and strong cyclic (the original paper) [SEC].** Cimatti, Pistore, Roveri & Traverso, "Weak, strong, and strong cyclic planning via symbolic model checking", *Artificial Intelligence* 147(1–2):35–84, 2003, DOI 10.1016/S0004-3702(02)00374-0. The abstract "formally characterizes different planning problems, where solutions have a chance of success (weak planning), are guaranteed to achieve the goal (strong planning) or achieve the goal with iterative trial-and-error strategies (strong cyclic planning)". For strong cyclic plans, "all the executions … always have a possibility of terminating and, when they do, they are guaranteed to achieve the goal". The algorithms are proven correct and complete and implemented in MBP with BDDs. — [FBK record](https://cris.fbk.eu/handle/11582/353); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(02)00374-0)
- **The same notions as stated in modern FOND work [FT].** A policy maps states to actions and is *closed* if it is defined on every state it reaches. A *weak plan* reaches the goal under some realisation of the outcomes and is typically not closed. A *strong plan* is a closed partial policy guaranteed to reach the goal in finitely many steps. A *strong cyclic plan* is a closed partial policy that embodies a weak plan for every reachable state, and "provides a solution to the FOND planning under an assumption of fairness (Cimatti et al. 2003)". — [Muise, McIlraith & Beck, PRP Rebooted, AAAI-24](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.11675)
- **Strong cyclic planning revisited [SEC].** Daniele, Traverso & Vardi, "Strong Cyclic Planning Revisited", ECP-99, LNCS 1809, pp. 35–48 (cited in PRP Rebooted). — [PRP Rebooted references](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.11675)
- **Solvability of fully observable nondeterministic problems [FT].** Algorithms exist for finding conditional plans, with loops, "in polynomial time in the size of the state space, see for example (Cimatti et al. 2003)". — [Rintanen 2004, proof of Thm. 11](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Reducing contingent planning to FOND [FT].** Contingent problems "cannot be translated into classical problems but can be translated into non-deterministic fully observable problems X(P)". Strong solutions of X(P) are policy trees over states instead of beliefs. A classical relaxation X+(P) (no deletes, no preconditions, no nondeterministic actions) yields heuristics. — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf). Muise, Belle & McIlraith later compute contingent plans with the FOND planner PRP: "Computing Contingent Plans via Fully Observable Non-Deterministic Planning", AAAI-14, pp. 2322–2329 [SEC, cited in PRP Rebooted](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.11675).
- **Offline vs online [FT].** The CLG planner "can be used on-line or off-line. In the first case, the non-deterministic actions obs(L) in X(P) are applied by selecting one of outcomes randomly; in the second, both outcomes are considered." Online mode solves problems where the full plan is infeasible: the largest offline doors instance (doors-9) needs a policy tree with more than 46,000 actions, while online CLG solves doors-13. — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf)
- **Online replanning with a classical planner (K-replanner) [FT].** For simple, connected problems, a policy solves P iff for every reachable belief b, Π(b) is the *executable prefix* of a classical plan for K(P)[s_b]. In K(P), sensors become "assumption" actions. The prefix runs until an assumption is revealed, which formalises "planning under optimism" (as in free-space assumption planning). Each prefix either reaches the goal or makes a sensor informative (Proposition 9). — [Bonet & Geffner 2011](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- **Weak online planning with postdiction [FT].** In robotics practice, strong planning can be inappropriate when actions may fail through unmodelled abnormalities. The h-approximation (HPX, ASP-based) planner instead does incremental *weak* planning interleaved with execution, uses sensing for postdiction, and has plan existence in NP. — [Eppe & Bhatt, "Narrative based Postdictive Reasoning for Cognitive Robotics", arXiv 1306.0665 (2013)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1306.0665)
- **USP-related work [SEC].** Andrés, de Barros & Delgado, "Human-aware Contingent Planning", *Fundamenta Informaticae* 174(1):63–81, 2020 (cited in PRP Rebooted). Leliane N. de Barros and Karina V. Delgado are at USP. — [PRP Rebooted references](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2312.11675)

### Inferences
- For a hierarchical architecture with high-level contingent planning over a three-valued KB and low-level RL, the *online / closed-loop* view (CLG online mode, K-replanner) is the natural fit. The low-level RL policy plays the role of executing actions and producing observations, and the planner replans on the updated KB. Strong cyclic semantics (fairness) matches RL-executed actions that may fail and be retried.
- Weak, strong and strong cyclic were defined for full observability (FOND). Under partial observability they lift to belief space: Bertoli et al. 2006 for strong; Rintanen's definition gives the strong-cyclic analogue.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of Cimatti et al. 2003. The exact formal definitions (state-action tables, the fixpoint characterisations) come from the abstract and from PRP Rebooted's restatement, not from the original.
- Bertoli et al. 2006 (AIJ 170) was not opened. Its exact formalisation of strong planning under partial observability is unverified.
- I did not find a primary source here on the strong cyclic notion under partial observability for MBP. Bertoli et al. had MBP work on this, but it was not checked.

## 4. Complexity results

### Takeaway
For propositional (succinctly represented) problems, *plan existence* is:

- PSPACE-complete for classical planning (Bylander 1994).
- EXPTIME-complete for FOND / full observability (Littman 1997).
- EXPSPACE-complete for conformant planning, with no observability and either nondeterminism or multiple initial states (Haslum & Jonsson 2000). This holds even with deterministic actions.
- **2-EXPTIME-complete for contingent planning with partial observability and nondeterministic actions** (Rintanen 2004).
- EXPSPACE-complete for partial observability with deterministic actions (Rintanen 2004).

If plans are restricted to *polynomial length*, the problems drop into the polynomial hierarchy or PSPACE:

- Classical: NP-complete.
- Conformant: Σ2P-complete (Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo 2000; Turner 2002 for "proper" domains), or Σ3P-complete for general domains (Turner 2002; Eiter et al.).
- Conditional plans with a bounded number of sensing actions: Σ2P-complete (Baral et al.).
- Sound three-valued approximations: NP.

Probabilistic variants with success probability below 1 are undecidable without observability (Madani, Hanks & Condon 2003).

### Cited Findings
**Unbounded plan existence (Rintanen 2004) [FT]**
- Abstract: "for conditional planning with partial observability the problem of testing existence of plans with success probability 1 is 2-EXP-complete. This result completes the complexity picture for non-probabilistic propositional planning." The proofs show how lack of full observability "allows the encoding of exponential space Turing machines" and how branching "corresponds to the move to a complexity class defined in terms of alternation". AEXPSPACE = 2-EXP. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- **Table 1 of the paper (plan existence; multiple initial states):**

  | | deterministic | non-deterministic |
  |---|---|---|
  | full observability | PSPACE | EXP |
  | no observability | EXPSPACE | EXPSPACE |
  | partial observability | EXPSPACE | 2-EXP |

  "Restriction to only one initial state affects the deterministic unobservable and partially observable planning problems only: they both come down to PSPACE from EXPSPACE." — [Rintanen 2004, Tables 1–2](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- Membership in 2-EXP: view the partially observable problem as a fully observable nondeterministic problem over belief states. The belief space is doubly exponential, and FOND algorithms run in time polynomial in the state space (Thm 14). — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- EXP-hardness of FOND is Littman (1997, AAAI-97, pp. 748–754), via a reduction from the game G4 (Stockmeyer & Chandra 1979). Rintanen gives a new proof via EXP = APSPACE. With full observability and *deterministic* operators the problem is in PSPACE (Thm 18). — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- Rintanen's semantics is goal reachability with probability 1, and plans may loop. Probabilistic optimality measures such as expected cost "can easily be seen to make the plan existence problem undecidable". — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)

**Conformant (Haslum & Jonsson) [FT]**
- "The main result of this paper is that the non-branching plan existence problem in unobservable domains with an expressive operator formalism is EXPSPACE-complete." Hardness is by reduction from universality of regular expressions with exponentiation. Membership follows because plans need at most 2^(2^n) steps, since the search is over state sets. They also show that polynomial-length plan existence is in PSPACE (it was open whether it is PSPACE-hard). Two restricted classes are PSPACE-complete: deterministic, context-independent operators with an arbitrary incomplete initial state, and T/F/U-preserving operators. Finally, a limited form of branching (IF φ1 THEN A1; …) and partial observability can be encoded into unobservable problems. — [Haslum & Jonsson, ECP-99 / LNAI 1809 (2000)](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/aiics/publications/ECP-1999-Some-Results-Complexity.pdf)
- Citation note: Albore et al. cite it as "Haslum and Jonsson, 1999" (ECP-99). Rintanen cites it as 2000, the year of the Springer LNAI 1809 volume. Both refer to the same paper. — [Rintanen 2004 refs](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf); [Albore et al. 2009 refs](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf)

**Polynomial / bounded-length variants [FT]**
- Classical planning with plan length bounded by a polynomial is NP-complete. — [Baral et al. TR](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/2001/tr01-01f.pdf)
- Conformant planning with an incomplete initial state (deterministic actions, language A) and polynomial-length plans: "In [Baral, Kreinovich, Trejo 2000] it is shown that (conformant) planning with respect to incomplete initial states and goals that are propositional formulas is Σ2P-complete." This holds also for LTL goals. The 0-approximation version is NP-complete and sound. — [Baral et al. TR, Thms 3.3 & 3.5](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/2001/tr01-01f.pdf)
- Conditional planning with sensing (knowledge goals): "with incomplete information about the initial state and with k-limited sensing actions checking the existence of a k-bounded plan is Σ2P-complete". A k-bounded plan has at most k sensing actions, and a k-limited sensing action reveals at most k fluents. This is described as a straightforward extension of Theorem 6 of Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo 2000. The 0-approximate version is NP-complete (Thm 4.3). — [Baral et al. TR, Thms 4.1 & 4.3](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/2001/tr01-01f.pdf)
- Turner (JELIA 2002, "Polynomial-length planning spans the polynomial hierarchy"): conformant plan existence with polynomial length is Σ3P-complete in general, Σ3P-hard already for length 1. It is Σ2P-complete when transitions are polynomially decidable ("proper"), and Σ2P-hard for length 1 in deterministic domains. Eiter et al.'s language K gives the same results: secure (conformant) planning with fixed length is Σ3P-complete in general and Σ2P-complete for proper domains; with arbitrary length it is NEXPTIME-complete in their (concurrent-action, ASP-based) setting. — [Eiter et al., arXiv cs/0112006, §6.3 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0112006)
- Rintanen notes that the complexity of conditional planning with "polynomial plan size, polynomial execution length, constant execution length" was studied in Rintanen (1999, "Constructing conditional plans by a theorem-prover", JAIR 10:323–352), Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo (2000) and Turner (2002). — [Rintanen 2004, Related Work](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- HPX (h-approximation) puts plan existence in NP, "For alternative PWS based formalisms, plan existence is Σ2P-complete, e.g. (Baral, Kreinovich, and Trejo 2000)". — [Eppe & Bhatt 2013](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1306.0665)

**Probabilistic / POMDP side [FT/SEC]**
- Probabilistic planning without observability with success threshold c is undecidable (Madani, Hanks & Condon, AIJ 147(1–2):5–34, 2003), via the emptiness problem of probabilistic finite automata. Undecidability holds for every fixed c ∈ (0,1). For c = 1 the belief space is finite and the problem is decidable. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- Littman, Goldsmith & Mundhenk, "The Computational Complexity of Probabilistic Planning", JAIR 9:1–36, 1998 (arXiv cs/9808101). They study plan evaluation and existence for short plans: totally ordered, acyclic, looping and partially ordered plans, in flat and propositional representations. Results range from PL and P to NP, PP, NP^PP and PSPACE. They introduce the NP^PP-complete problem E-MAJSAT. — [JAIR page](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10208); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9808101)
- Mundhenk, Goldsmith, Lusena & Allender (JACM 47(4):681–720, 2000) analyse finite-horizon MDP/POMDP policy existence. For history-dependent POMDPs represented by circuits with exponential horizons, the fully observable, unobservable and general cases are EXP, NEXP and EXPSPACE. Rintanen notes these are lower than his EXP / EXPSPACE / 2-EXP "because of the exponential horizon length". — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- Haslum & Jonsson compare their EXPSPACE result with probabilistic results: finding an optimal m-horizon history-dependent policy for a succinct POMDP is EXPSPACE-complete, while the unobservable and fully observable variants are easier. They describe the corresponding probabilistic problem as NEXPTIME-complete and "thus presumably easier", and conclude that "nondeterministic choice contains less information than probabilistic choice". — [Haslum & Jonsson 2000](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/aiics/publications/ECP-1999-Some-Results-Complexity.pdf)

**Infinite executions [SEC]**
- Rintanen, "Complexity of Conditional Planning under Partial Observability and Infinite Executions", ECAI 2012, pp. 678–683. It studies maintenance goals and recurrence (Büchi-style, "visit goal infinitely often") under different observability assumptions, with worst-case rather than expected cost. — [PDF (not fetched)](https://users.aalto.fi/~rintanj1/papers/Rintanen12ecai2.pdf); [dblp](https://dblp.org/pid/65/3601)

### Inferences
- Short summary for the report: removing observability adds an exponential in *space*, adding branching over nondeterminism adds *alternation*, and both together give AEXPSPACE = 2-EXPTIME. This is Rintanen's own explanation.
- For the student's architecture, the polynomial-horizon, bounded-sensing results (Σ2P) and the NP results for three-valued approximations justify using a knowledge-level (known-true / known-false / unknown) KB at the high level. It trades completeness for a drop from Σ2P (or 2-EXP unbounded) to NP, while staying sound.
- Contingent planning is decidable (2-EXP), while DEL multi-agent epistemic planning is in general undecidable (Aucher & Bolander 2013, cited in EPDDL). The contrast is worth stating, and it motivates the DEL fragments that EPDDL lets planners declare.

### Gaps
- I could not open Baral, Kreinovich & Trejo (AIJ 122:241–267, 2000) directly. Its results (Σ2P for conformant; Σ2P for bounded-sensing conditional plans; NP for 0-approximation) are taken from the authors' follow-up TR and from Eiter et al.'s summary. I could not confirm a widely repeated claim that "polynomial-length conditional planning with sensing is PSPACE-complete" in that paper. Treat it as unverified.
- The exact theorems of Rintanen ECAI-2012 (infinite executions) were not retrieved.
- I did not verify complexity results specific to the *size* of contingent plans (polynomial-size vs polynomial-depth trees) beyond Rintanen's pointer to Rintanen 1999 / Turner 2002.

## 5. Relation to POMDPs and to AND-OR search

### Takeaway
Contingent planning is the *qualitative* (set-based, probability-free) counterpart of POMDP planning. Beliefs are sets of states instead of distributions. The objective is goal achievement with certainty (or probability 1) instead of expected reward or cost. The belief space is therefore finite, so the problem is decidable (2-EXP), while the probabilistic threshold versions are undecidable. Algorithmically, contingent planning is AND-OR search: OR nodes are the planner's action choices, and AND nodes are the observation outcomes or nondeterministic effects, which all must be handled. The search runs either in belief space or, after compilation, in a FOND state space.

### Cited Findings
- Bonet & Geffner (AIPS-2000) formalised classical, conformant and contingent planning uniformly as heuristic search in belief space. Belief spaces range from sets of states (non-probabilistic) to probability distributions (POMDP), solved with RTDP-style algorithms in the GPT planner. — [UW CSE574 reading list and reviews (secondary)](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse574/03sp/06-options.html); [Bonet & Geffner, "Planning with incomplete information as heuristic search in belief space", AIPS-2000, pp. 52–61 (as cited in Bonet & Geffner 2011)](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- Belief-space search "is the mainstream approach in both contingent … and POMDP planning [Kaelbling et al.]". This refers to Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra, "Planning and acting in partially observable stochastic domains", AIJ 101:99–134 (published 1998; cited as 1999 in the source). — [Bonet & Geffner 2011](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/blai-ijcai11.pdf)
- Probabilities vs sets: with success probability 1, "the exact probabilities of nondeterministic events do not matter, and the uncertainty about the current state can be represented in terms of a set of possible current states". This is why the problem is decidable, while the probabilistic threshold problem is undecidable. — [Rintanen 2004](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- AND-OR correspondence: in the EXP-hardness proof, ∀ states of an alternating TM become nondeterministic operators and ∃ states become the planner's choice of deterministic operators. This gives "an exact correspondence with the AND-OR tree evaluation in the definition of alternating Turing machines". — [Rintanen 2004, Thm 10 proof](https://gki.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/rintanen-icaps04.pdf)
- Contingent planning as "a non-deterministic (AND/OR) search problem in belief space". — [Albore et al. 2009](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~hector.geffner/www.dtic.upf.edu/~hgeffner/alex-ijcai09.pdf)
- Geffner & Bonet's textbook separates "Planning with Sensing: Logical Models" (contingent, set-based beliefs) from "MDP Planning" and "POMDP Planning: Stochastic Actions and Partial Feedback". The publisher blurb stresses that "all models, whether featuring uncertainty and feedback or not, are intractable in the worst case when represented in compact form". — [Geffner & Bonet, *A Concise Introduction to Models and Methods for Automated Planning*, Morgan & Claypool, Synthesis Lectures on AI & ML #22, 2013; Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-01564-9](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-031-01564-9)

### Inferences
- For an RL-based low level: if the low level has stochastic outcomes, the high-level contingent plan is a qualitative abstraction of the corresponding POMDP. Strong-cyclic contingent solutions correspond to POMDP policies that reach the goal with probability 1 when every outcome has non-zero probability, which is Rintanen's probabilistic reading of his plan definition.
- The DEL view adds higher-order (multi-agent) knowledge on top of the qualitative belief representation. Single-agent DEL with sensing events is essentially contingent planning on Kripke models.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary source that explicitly states "contingent planning = POMDP with set-valued beliefs and goal-reachability criterion" as a formal reduction. The link is well established through Bonet & Geffner 2000 and Rintanen 2004 as cited above, but no single theorem states it.
- AO*/LAO* (Hansen & Zilberstein 2001, AIJ 129) as the standard AND-OR algorithms for these problems were not verified in this session.
