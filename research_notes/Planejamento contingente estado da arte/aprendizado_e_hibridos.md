# Contingent / partially observable planning × learning (RL, model learning, POMDP-based RL) — positioning a hybrid "symbolic planning with partial information (high level) + RL (low level)" architecture, up to Oct 2026

Scope note: compiled 2026-10-08. Items marked **[not verified this session]** are cited from prior knowledge (canonical URL given) but their content was not re-checked via search/fetch in this session; the report writer should treat them as lower-confidence. arXiv IDs with a 26xx prefix are 2026 preprints (mostly not yet peer reviewed unless a venue is stated).

## 1. Hybrid works combining symbolic planning (incl. under partial observability) with RL

### Takeaway
There is a well-established lineage (PEORL 2018 → SDRL 2019 → Illanes et al. 2020 "taskable RL" → reward machines 2018–2024 → planning-operator options 2022–2024 → logic-guided DRL 2026) showing that a symbolic high level gives large sample-efficiency gains and transfer to unseen tasks, but almost all of it assumes the *symbolic* level is fully observable; explicitly partially observable variants exist but are few (LRM 2019, Noisy RMs 2024, IBM PO-HRL 2024, Veronese et al. 2026), which is precisely the gap a "contingent planner on top of RL" architecture can claim.

### Cited Findings
**Planning-guided HRL (fully observable symbolic level)**
- PEORL (Yang, Lyu, Liu, Gustafson, IJCAI 2018, pp. 4860–4866): symbolic planner (ASP) output steers execution/learning of options; what is learned is fed back into the symbolic knowledge to improve later plans; reports faster policy search and more robust plans on standard HRL benchmarks. Note: it is IJCAI 2018, not AAAI 2019. — [IJCAI proceedings](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/675); [arXiv 1804.07779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.07779)
- SDRL (Lyu, Yang, Liu, Gustafson, AAAI 2019, 33(01):2970–2977): planner–controller–meta-controller architecture (subtask scheduling, data-driven subtask learning, subtask evaluation); symbolic actions are related to options, giving task-level interpretability; tested on Montezuma's Revenge first room where vanilla DQN often scores 0; claims improved data efficiency vs. state of the art. A separate short AAAI-19 paper "Logic-Based Sequential Decision-Making" describes the same framework — cite the right one. — [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4153); [arXiv 1811.00090](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00090)
- Illanes, Yan, Toro Icarte, McIlraith, "Symbolic Plans as High-Level Instructions for Reinforcement Learning", ICAPS 2020 (DOI 10.1609/icaps.v30i1.6750): high-level symbolic action models define final-state goal tasks and automatically produce reward functions; plans guide HRL; learns policies for unseen tasks "up to an order of magnitude faster" than standard RL; formal characterization of *taskable RL environments* with sufficient conditions for optimality notions (min total cost, max probability of reaching goal). Earlier version: KR2ML 2019 "Leveraging Symbolic Planning Models in HRL". — [ICAPS proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6750); [PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6750/6750-40-9979-1-10-20200522.pdf); [KR2ML 2019](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~lillanes/papers/IllanesYTM-kr2ml2019-leveraging.pdf)
- Lee, Katz, Agravante, Liu, Nangue Tasse, Klinger, Sohrabi (IBM), "Hierarchical RL with AI Planning Models" (arXiv 2022; NeurIPS 2023 GenPlan workshop): defines *options from AI-planning operators* by connecting the planning and RL transition systems; option policies learned with model-free DRL + intrinsic rewards for consistency between the two transition systems; MiniGrid suite with increasing exploration difficulty and N-rooms; hand-written PDDL "MazeRooms" abstraction ignores agent pose, exact key/ball/goal location; door state abstracted to locked/unlocked; code: github.com/IBM/parl_agents. — [arXiv 2203.00669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00669); [NeurIPS 2023 workshop page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/79903)
- Same IBM group, "Partially Observable Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning with AI Planning (Student Abstract)", AAAI 2024 — the closest direct precedent for a "partially observable planner over RL options" in MiniGrid. Contents (method, results) could not be fetched in this session. — [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30504); [IBM page](https://research.ibm.com/publications/partially-observable-hierarchical-reinforcement-learning-with-ai-planning)

**Reward machines (RMs) as the automaton/plan interface to RL, including partial observability**
- Toro Icarte et al., "Learning Reward Machines for Partially Observable RL", NeurIPS 2019: RMs act as *memory* in POMDPs; RM learning framed as discrete optimization (find an RM so that combining optimal *memoryless* policies per RM state is optimal), solved by local search; needs a finite set of event detectors (vocabulary); significantly outperforms A3C, PPO and ACER on three partially observable domains. Journal version: arXiv 2112.09477 (2021). — [NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/532435c44bec236b471a47a88d63513d-Abstract.html); [PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~rntoro/docs/LRM_paper.pdf); [journal-length version](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2112.09477)
- Li, Chen, Klassen, Vaezipoor, Toro Icarte, McIlraith, "Reward Machines for Deep RL in Noisy and Uncertain Environments", NeurIPS 2024: the labelling function (vocabulary grounding) is uncertain due to partial observability/noisy sensors; problem cast as a POMDP; proposes algorithms that keep a *belief over RM states* (e.g., "IBU" belief update; running example "Gold Mining" where the probability of having gold rises 0.3 → 0.51 after repeated digging) and exposes pitfalls of naive approaches. Code: github.com/andrewli77/reward-machines-noisy-environments. — [alphaXiv 2406.00120](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2406.00120)
- Original RM paper: Toro Icarte et al., "Using Reward Machines for High-Level Task Specification and Decomposition in RL", ICML 2018 **[not verified this session]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/icarte18a.html)
- 2025–2026 RM extensions (abstract-level only): Stochastic RMs (arXiv 2510.14837, Oct 2025); Symbolic RMs (arXiv 2603.03068, Mar 2026); RMs + Signal Temporal Logic (arXiv 2604.14440, Apr 2026). — [2510.14837](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.14837); [2603.03068](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.03068); [2604.14440](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.14440)

**Recent (2025–2026) neuro-symbolic RL in gridworlds**
- Veronese, Farinelli, Meli, "Sample-Efficient Neurosymbolic Deep RL", AAMAS 2026 (arXiv 2601.02850, v2 Apr 2026): partial policies learned as logical (ASP) rules on small instances are transferred to larger ones; online reasoning (i) biases exploration and (ii) rescales Q-values (SR-DQN); OfficeWorld and MiniGrid DoorKey (8×8 and 16×16, 1–4 keys), fully and partially observable; beats a reward-machine baseline (RM-DQN); symbolic reasoning overhead is small: e.g., +4 min (5%) on DK 8×8/1 key over 3M steps, +8 min (4.82%) on DK 16×16 over 10M steps, +1.3–2.5% on OfficeWorld. A KR 2026 version also exists. — [alphaXiv 2601.02850](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.02850); [KR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.kr.org/2026/109/kr2026-0109-veronese-et-al.pdf)
- "Learning from Less: Guiding Deep RL with Differentiable Symbolic Planning" (Dylan; arXiv 2505.11661, May 2025): differentiable symbolic planner as adaptive reward model and composer of policy primitives; MiniGrid DoorKey variant with multiple solution paths; baselines A2C and PPO. Details not checked. — [arXiv 2505.11661](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11661)
- "Managing Action Preconditions in Neuro-Symbolic RL: Three Placement Strategies for Embodied Agents" (arXiv 2609.16056, Sep 2026) — abstract only. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16056)
- BlendRL (TU Darmstadt, arXiv 2410.11689, Oct 2024; ICLR 2025 per my recollection **[venue not verified]**): mixes symbolic (logic) and neural policies. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.11689)
- Neuro-symbolic skill discovery for *conditional* multi-level planning (Cambridge/Bogazici, arXiv 2410.10045, Oct 2024) — learns high-level symbolic skills usable in conditional (contingent-like) plans. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2410.10045)
- NetHack: in the NeurIPS 2021 NetHack Challenge, symbolic agents outperformed neural ones by >4× median score; neural agents trained from the winning symbolic agent improved +127% (offline) / +25% (online) over prior neural policies, and "mere scaling is insufficient" to close the gap (Piterbarg, Pinto, Fergus, NeurIPS 2023). — [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/764ba7236fb63743014fafbd87dd4f0e-Abstract.html)

**Minecraft / LLM + planner hybrids**
- Plan4MC (Yuan et al., PKU/BAAI, arXiv 2303.16563; NeurIPS 2023 Workshop? **[venue not verified]**): RL-learned basic skills (Finding, Manipulation, Crafting) with intrinsic rewards + an LLM-built skill graph searched at run time; v2 reports 40 tasks (v1: 24), claims the most sample-efficient demonstration-free RL method on Minecraft Tech Tree tasks; ablation shows removing the separately learned "Find" skill hurts — a direct analogue of "exploration/sensing" being a first-class skill. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16563); [code](https://github.com/PKU-RL/Plan4MC)
- DEPS (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023): LLM planner with Describe–Explain–Plan–Select; replans on subgoal failure, CLIP-like describer of state; selector ranks parallel subgoals by estimated steps. — [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/6b8dfb8c0c12e6fafc6c256cb08a5ca7-Abstract.html)
- JARVIS-1 (arXiv 2311.05997): memory-augmented multimodal LM planner from pixels, converts observations to text; Voyager relies on privileged state (nearby object coordinates, inventory, path planner), i.e., it sidesteps partial observability. — [JARVIS-1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05997); [survey noting Voyager's privileged info](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19479)
- "Iterative Formalization and Planning in Partially Observable Environments" (Drexel/USC, arXiv 2505.13126, May 2025): LLM writes/updates PDDL as the agent explores a partially observable environment, then a planner solves it. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.13126)
- "When in Doubt, Plan It Out" / PACT (arXiv 2606.16995, Jun 2026): hybrid where a small LM deliberates and commits to plans that a reactive RL policy follows. Abstract only. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16995)

**Belief-space / POMDP planning hybrids with learning (2024–2026, mostly robotics)**
- PO-PDDL: learns *symbolic POMDPs* from visual demonstrations (SJTU, arXiv 2606.15654, Jun 2026). — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.15654)
- "Bridging Learned Visual Perception and Symbolic Belief-Space Planning" (Technion, arXiv 2609.16884, Sep 2026). — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16884)
- PriPG-RL: privileged planner guides RL under partial observability (arXiv 2604.08036, Apr 2026); Imagine-TAMP: TAMP under partial observability deciding to observe vs act (arXiv 2609.20396); interleaved POMDP planning for multi-object search in unknown multi-room homes (arXiv 2607.10437); Hierarchical object-oriented POMDP planning for rearrangement (arXiv 2412.01348). — [2604.08036](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.08036); [2609.20396](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20396); [2607.10437](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10437); [2412.01348](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.01348)
- NS-POMDPs (2023): neural perception of continuous state + symbolic decision making, theoretical framework. — [arXiv 2306.17639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17639v1)

### Inferences
- The defensible novelty claim is not "planning + RL" (crowded since 2018) but "*contingent/belief-level* symbolic planning (sensing actions, branching on observations) over RL skills, in procedurally generated open-ended environments". Closest competitors to position against explicitly: IBM PO-HRL (AAAI 2024 SA), LRM/Noisy RMs (Toronto), Veronese et al. 2026, Plan4MC (find-skill ≈ sensing).
- Reward machines are the natural "bridge object": a contingent plan (policy tree/graph over observations) can be compiled into an RM-like automaton whose transitions are observation outcomes; this gives a principled way to plug the planner into QRM/CRM-style counterfactual RL, and Noisy RMs (2024) already provide the belief-handling machinery for uncertain labelling.
- Note that most planning-HRL works abstract away exactly the things that are partially observable (e.g., IBM's MiniGrid abstraction drops key/goal positions); a contingent high level that *reasons about where unknown objects might be* is a concrete differentiator.

## 2. Learning action models / sensing models under partial observability

### Takeaway
Action-model learning under partial observability goes from exact logical filtering (SLAF 2008) to compilation-to-planning (FAMA 2019), safety-guaranteed learning (SAM family, AAAI 2024), probabilistic/noisy and lifted partial-trace learners (2024–2025), and in 2026 neurosymbolic learners from occluded images (NeSyAM); *online* learning coupled with contingent planning remains thin, and learning *sensing* models specifically is rarely addressed.

### Cited Findings
- SLAF — Amir & Chang, "Learning Partially Observable Deterministic Action Models", JAIR 33 (2008) 349–402: exact online learning of deterministic action models under partial observability by updating a propositional *transition belief formula* (simultaneous learning and filtering). — [arXiv 1401.3437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3437)
- FAMA — Aineto, Jiménez Celorrio, Onaindia, "Learning action models with minimal observability", Artificial Intelligence 275 (2019) 104–137: compiles STRIPS model learning into classical planning; works when actions are partially/totally unobservable and intermediate states only partially given. — [AIJ listing](https://datalearner.com:8443/academic/journal-papers/0004-3702/volumes-and-issues/461/paper-detail/2554); reference also in [NeSyAM 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25766)
- Le, Juba, Stern, "Learning Safe Action Models with Partial Observability", AAAI 2024, 38(18):20159–20167: two algorithms learning PDDL models guaranteed *safe* (plans sound) even with partially observable states; often better than FAMA. — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29995)
- Other recent learners (from NeSyAM's related work): Lamanna & Serafini, noisy traces, probabilistic approach (ICAPS 2024); Aineto & Scala, learning with guarantees (KR 2024); Balyo et al., state traces without action parameters (KR 2024); Lamanna, Serafini, Saetti, Gerevini, Traverso, "Lifted action models learning from partial traces", AIJ 339 (2025) 104256; Xi, Gould, Thiébaux, neuro-symbolic learning of lifted action models from visual traces (ICAPS 2024) and from unsupervised visual traces (ICAPS 2026). — [NeSyAM reference list](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25766)
- NeSyAM — Kikaj, De Smet, Marra, De Raedt (KU Leuven), "Neurosymbolic Action Model Learning under Partial Observability", arXiv 2609.25766 (22 Sep 2026): action-conditioned HMM over symbolic states with neural perception/emission; exact constrained STRIPS transitions instead of fuzzy relaxation; shows prior SOTA (rosame-i) is a mean-field approximation that needs complete fully observable traces. Six visual domains × three observation regimes: relevant-role recovery NeSyAM 90/90 (full), 88.33 (random occlusion), 89.33 (action occlusion) vs rosame-i 87.00 → 61.33 / 68.00; but more false positives on "not-involved" roles (positive-trace identifiability problem: pre vs not-involved). — [alphaXiv 2609.25766](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25766)
- Online learning: OLAM (Lamanna et al., KEPS 2021 / IJCAI 2021) learns action models online with the agent choosing actions, but *requires full observability* and no sensor noise. — [KEPS 2021 paper](https://ai-icaps.dmi.unibas.ch/workshops/KEPS/Papers/KEPS_2021_paper_10.pdf)
- Online contingent planning with known models: HCP (Maliah, Brafman, Karpas, Shani, ICAPS 2014) picks the next useful sensing action using landmark heuristics and classical planning; solves many more problems than translation-based online contingent planners, mostly faster. — [ICAPS 2014](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13632)
- Learning *symbolic abstractions from skills* (Konidaris, Kaelbling, Lozano-Pérez, "From skills to symbols", JAIR 61 (2018) 215–289) and latent-space classical planning (Asai et al., JAIR 74 (2022)) are the canonical "learn the planning model from low-level experience" references. — cited via [NeSyAM refs](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25766)
- LLM-based model formalization under partial observability: PSALM-V (Zhu et al., arXiv 2506.20097, 2025) induces action semantics with LLMs in interactive partially observed visual environments; iterative PDDL formalization (arXiv 2505.13126). — [NeSyAM refs](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25766); [2505.13126](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.13126)

### Inferences
- For a hybrid architecture, an attractive validation story is: (a) hand-written high-level model as an upper bound; (b) model learned (SAM-style safe learning, or NeSyAM-style from observations) as the realistic setting; (c) report how planner performance degrades with learned-model errors. Safe-model learning (Le et al.) is particularly relevant because unsound learned models produce plans the RL layer cannot execute.
- Sensing-action models (what an observation reveals) are almost never learned in this literature; in gridworlds the sensing model is often given by the environment's egocentric view, which can be hand-coded — worth stating as an assumption.

### Gaps
- Found no work that learns action *and sensing* models online while doing contingent planning (closest: OLAM for online/full obs; SLAF for partial obs/no planning). No source found; appears open.
- Wikipedia remarks that action-model learning is often outside main ICAPS tracks — weak source, not used as evidence. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_model_learning)

## 3. Contingent planning vs POMDP; deep-RL POMDP baselines; how hybrids are compared empirically

### Takeaway
Contingent planning is the qualitative (possibilistic, outcome-sets without probabilities) end of a ladder whose quantitative end is goal-POMDPs; qualitative models win when probabilities are unknown/hard to specify or the POMDP is too large, and recent work (Shani's group, 2023–2024) imports contingent/classical-planning heuristics into POMDP solvers (POMCP, RTDP-Bel) with big gains on long-horizon domains. Deep-RL POMDP baselines are DRQN and recurrent PPO (GRU best in POPGym); hybrids are typically compared on sample efficiency curves, success rate and transfer to larger/unseen layouts.

### Cited Findings
- Bonet & Geffner (AIPS 2000) "Planning with Incomplete Information as Heuristic Search in Belief Space": classical ⊂ conformant ⊂ contingent ⊂ probabilistic contingent (POMDP) ladder; problems solved via belief-space search / RTDP. — [course summary](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse574/03sp/Messages/paper6/0001.html) (secondary)
- Qualitative contingent model "specifies the possible outcome states without their likelihood"; this is an advantage "where it is difficult to specify a richer, quantitative model, or when such models are too complex to solve" (Shani et al., QDec-POMDP paper). — [PDF](https://tzin.bgu.ac.il/~shanigu/Publications/QDEC-POMDP.8.pdf)
- Rollout heuristics for online stochastic contingent planning (arXiv 2310.02345, 2023): model goal POMDPs as contingent planning problems so classical-planning heuristics guide POMCP rollouts; "heuristics provide much leverage, especially in complex domains that require a long planning horizon". — [arXiv 2310.02345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02345)
- Heuristics for Partially Observable Stochastic Contingent Planning (arXiv 2410.05870, 2024; Annals of Math & AI per Springer DOI 10.1007/s10472-024-09947-5): RTDP-Bel guided by a relaxed-space heuristic accounting for value of information and stochastic effects. — [arXiv 2410.05870](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05870v1); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10472-024-09947-5)
- DRQN (Hausknecht & Stone, AAAI Fall Symposium 2015): LSTM replaces first FC layer of DQN; with single frames it integrates info over time and matches DQN on Atari and flickering-Atari POMDPs; *no systematic advantage in learning* vs frame stacking, but adapts better when observation quality changes at test time. — [arXiv 1507.06527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06527)
- POPGym (Morad et al., ICLR 2023): 15 POMDP tasks with difficulty levels and procedural generation, 13 memory-model baselines in RLlib; GRU (PPO-GRU) was the best-performing memory model; caveat: RLlib PPO has non-standard implementation choices. Follow-up POPGym Arcade (arXiv 2503.01450, 2025) has pixel MDP/POMDP twin variants — useful for isolating the cost of partial observability. — [POPGym PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/popgym/1.0.0); [POPGym Arcade](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01450v2); [review](https://liner.com/review/popgym-benchmarking-partially-observable-reinforcement-learning)
- Other memory baselines: Fast and Forgetful Memory (arXiv 2310.04128), S4/S5 SSMs for in-context RL (arXiv 2303.03982), Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (arXiv 2306.09459). — [2310.04128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.04128); [2303.03982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03982); [2306.09459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09459)
- Empirical comparison patterns in hybrids: LRM vs A3C/PPO/ACER on PO domains (learning curves; LRM significantly better) — [NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/532435c44bec236b471a47a88d63513d-Abstract.html); Illanes et al.: time to learn policies for *unseen* tasks vs standard RL (≈10× faster) — [ICAPS 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6750); Veronese et al.: train on small grids, test on larger grids with more keys, vs DQN and RM-DQN, plus wall-clock overhead — [2601.02850](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.02850); Plan4MC: per-task success over 30 episodes with shared seeds across methods, plus ablations — [arXiv 2303.16563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16563); NetHack: median game score vs symbolic/neural agents — [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/764ba7236fb63743014fafbd87dd4f0e-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested validation design against the examiners' two extremes:
  1. **vs (1) pure contingent planning**: give the contingent planner the same abstract model but *primitive/scripted* low-level execution (or exact grid-level contingent planning); show the planner alone fails/scales poorly when low-level dynamics are continuous/stochastic or when the grounding is learned, while the hybrid retains the planner's high-level guarantees (plan validity, interpretability).
  2. **vs (2) POMDP RL**: PPO-GRU/LSTM (POPGym-style), DRQN, and a transformer/SSM memory agent, plus RM-based agents (CRM/QRM, LRM) as structured baselines; measure success rate vs environment steps (sample efficiency), zero-shot / few-shot success on held-out procedurally generated layouts and on larger maps, and long-horizon tasks (#subgoals).
  3. **Ablations**: conformant (no sensing) vs contingent high level; oracle vs learned abstraction/labelling function; noisy labelling (Noisy RM setup).
- Claimed hybrid advantages that the literature supports: sample efficiency (Illanes; LRM; Veronese), transfer/generalization to bigger or unseen instances (Veronese; Illanes unseen tasks), interpretability (SDRL), robustness to sparse reward/long horizons (SDRL Montezuma; NetHack symbolic dominance). Likely disadvantages to acknowledge: need for a symbolic vocabulary/labelling function (LRM requirement), brittleness to noisy grounding (Noisy RMs), modelling effort.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study of an off-the-shelf *contingent* planner vs a POMDP solver vs deep POMDP-RL on the same procedurally generated gridworld was found.
- Bonet's "Qualitative MDPs and POMDPs: An Order-Of-Magnitude Approximation" (arXiv 1301.0557) is a different notion of "qualitative" — not summarized. — [arXiv 1301.0557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0557)

## 4. Benchmarks and metrics for the comparison

### Takeaway
MiniGrid/BabyAI (egocentric partial view, DoorKey, multi-room), XLand-MiniGrid (procedural rulesets, JAX), Crafter/Craftax (open-ended achievements), POPGym (memory isolation), NetHack/MiniHack, OfficeWorld (RM standard), and MCU for Minecraft give a ladder from controllable to open-ended; achievements-based success rates and held-out-layout generalization are the standard metrics.

### Cited Findings
- XLand (DeepMind 2021): procedurally generated 3D worlds/games; agents from RGB + text goals; secondary coverage reports ~700k unique games, 4,000 worlds, 3.4M unique tasks, 200B training steps for the final generation (check primary). Primary: Open-Ended Learning Team, arXiv 2107.12808 **[not verified this session]**. — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/generally-capable-agents-emerge-from-open-ended-play/)
- XLand-MiniGrid (Nikulin et al., arXiv 2312.12044; NeurIPS 2024 D&B **[venue not verified]**): JAX, MiniGrid-style grid with procedurally generated rule/goal sets; benchmarks with up to 3 million unique rulesets. — [arXiv 2312.12044](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12044)
- Crafter (Hafner, arXiv 2109.06780, ICLR 2022 **[venue not verified]**): 2D Minecraft-like survival; evaluated by semantically meaningful achievements unlocked per episode; results in hours. — [arXiv 2109.06780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.06780); object-centric agents on Crafter generalization — [arXiv 2208.03374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03374)
- Craftax (Matthews et al., ICML 2024, PMLR v235): JAX; Craftax-Classic ≈250× faster than Crafter; full Craftax adds NetHack-inspired mechanics; global/episodic exploration and UED "fail to make material progress"; later reported best agents ≈18% of max reward (secondary). Multi-Agent Craftax (arXiv 2511.04904, Nov 2025). — [ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/matthews24a.html); [MA-Craftax](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04904)
- MCU — Minecraft Universe (Zheng et al., ICML 2025): 3,452 composable atomic tasks in 11 categories/41 subcategories, task composition for unlimited difficulty-varying tasks, VLM-based evaluation with 91.5% agreement with human ratings; SOTA foundation agents struggle as diversity/complexity grow; code github.com/CraftJarvis/MCU. — [ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/44393); [arXiv 2310.08367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08367)
- MiniGrid DoorKey / N-rooms used by IBM PO-HRL and Veronese et al.; OfficeWorld is the standard RM domain. — [arXiv 2203.00669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00669); [2601.02850](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.02850)

## 5. Multi-goal aspects: oversubscription, lexicographic/GUBS, goal-conditioned RL (HER) under partial observability

### Takeaway
GUBS (Freire & Delgado) gives a principled trade-off between probability-to-goal and cost-to-goal (generalizing the lexicographic "maximize probability then minimize cost" criterion) and now has RL variants (Q-learning-GUBS, 2024), making it a natural bridge between the planning and RL sides for multi-goal / dead-end-prone open-ended tasks; HER-style relabelling under partial observability is under-studied.

### Cited Findings
- GUBS introduced at AAMAS 2017 (Freire & Delgado): policies summarized by probability of reaching goal and expected cost; lexicographic approach (prioritize probability then minimize cost) does not allow a smooth trade-off; GUBS grounded in expected utility theory. — [AAMAS 2017 PDF](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p741.pdf)
- Freire, Delgado, Reis, ICAPS 2019: exact algorithm; optimal GUBS policies are non-Markovian. — [ICAPS 2019 PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/3470/3338/6519)
- Crispino, Freire, Delgado, Artificial Intelligence 2023: GUBS handles dead ends; eGUBS (exponential utility) with eGUBS-VI and eGUBS-AO*. UCT-GUBS (CLEI EJ, Aug 2024) online MCTS. — [FAPESP record](https://bv.fapesp.br/en/publicacao/240091); [CLEI EJ](https://www.clei.org/cleiej/index.php/cleiej/article/view/668)
- Polar, Delgado, Freire, "Reinforcement Learning with Utility-Based Semantic for Goals", BRACIS 2024: Q-learning-GUBS and Q-learning-eGUBS+Cmax trade off cost and probability without discounts or penalties. — [SBC](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/bracis/article/view/33605)
- Illanes et al. 2020 already characterize optimality notions for taskable RL including "maximize probability of reaching the goal" vs "minimize total cost" — a hook to connect GUBS to the planner-guided RL setting. — [ICAPS 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/6750)
- HER (Andrychowicz et al., NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1707.01495) **[not verified this session]**: relabels failed trajectories with achieved goals; requires access to achieved-goal and a goal-reward function (SB3/MathWorks docs). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495); [SB3 HER docs](https://tessl.io/registry/tessl/pypi-stable-baselines3/2.7.0/files/docs/her.md)
- Oversubscription planning (Smith, ICAPS 2004 "Choosing objectives in over-subscription planning") **[not verified this session; no URL retrieved, so treat as a lead to check, not a citation]**.

### Inferences
- In partially observable open-ended worlds, goals can become unreachable (dead ends) or uncertain; GUBS/eGUBS lets the high-level planner choose *which* goals to pursue with explicit probability–cost semantics, while the low level is trained goal-conditioned; this is a distinctive selling point vs both pure contingent planning (usually strong/weak solutions, no trade-off) and POMDP-RL (discounting conflates probability and cost).
- HER in POMDPs is non-trivial: the "achieved goal" must be observable to relabel, and relabelling interacts with recurrent hidden state; a symbolic labelling function (as in RMs) supplies observable achieved-goal predicates, which favors the hybrid.

### Gaps
- No search result found on HER combined with recurrent/partially observable agents; no work found combining GUBS with partial observability or with contingent planning.
- Oversubscription planning under partial observability: no source found in this session.
